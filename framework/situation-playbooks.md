# Situation playbooks · which files to read, what to ask, what to flag

**Purpose:** a routing aid. For a common client situation: what to ask first, which files to open in order, what the base can calculate, which flags to raise, and what the books do not cover. **It adds no new book advice;** process rules of ours are marked [our note]. Every step points to a concept file (framework/concepts/NN), the cross-reference map (map), or the flagged-claims list (claims).
**Status:** built by us from our own notes. Not independently checked. The situations are our choice, not the books'.
**Tag key:** [stated] · [derived] · [FLAG] · [our note].

## Rules for every playbook
1. Name the book and entry ID for each piece of advice (see 00-index.md).
2. If the books do not cover it, say so. Never invent book advice.
3. Never quote an author result as an expected outcome (claims).
4. Live ad accounts, client data and payments are risk areas. [our note: process rule, not from the books] Look first, show the exact change, wait for a yes before anything live, note how to undo it.
5. One question at a time to the client; start with the "Ask first" list.

---

## 1. "We don't get enough leads"
**Ask first:** which kind of lead is being counted (contactable / engaged / has problem and money, concept 01 s.2)? Which of the four methods are in use? More time or more money (LD-068)? Do they know cost per engaged lead and lead-to-sale rates?
**Read:** concept 12 → 01 (definitions) → the channel file by resource: time → 13 warm outreach, 14 content; money → 15 cold outreach, 16 paid ads → 20 (volume and weekly testing).
**The base can calculate:** cost per engaged lead by channel (concept 01 s.7); CAC = cost to acquire including non-ad costs such as sales (LD-060, LD-062).
**Flags:** LD-016 [stated]: doing the core four with too little skill or too little volume; [our note] the entry gives no numeric test for either. LD-001's "you're not advertising enough. Period." is a universal claim (claims s.5); do not use it as a diagnosis. Rule-of-100 units differ (count, minutes, dollars, concept 12): ask which. Contacting people at scale needs consent checks (concepts 13, 15).
**Not covered:** closing the sale (LD-003).

## 2. "Leads come in but they don't buy"
**Ask first:** counts at each step (leads → booked → showed → closed), who sells and how, price, what prospects say.
**Read:** concept 01 s.6 (compute the funnel from counts) → 02 (is the market normal or better?) → 04 (which Value Equation driver is weakest?) → 05 (offer build; solve perceived problems) → 09 (guarantee) → 03 (price).
**Check:** LD-062 lesson 1 and LD-080: do not blame advertising until the sales step is checked; CAC includes non-ad costs. OF-016 ranks the three levers (Market > Offer > Persuasion); [our note] fixing them in that order is our reading, and treating "persuasion" as "sales" is also ours.
**Flags:** the Value Equation gives a **relative** comparison only; no 1–10 scale, no weights, no pass mark (concept 04). Market rating is user judgment, with no numeric cut-offs (concept 02). Guarantees: model refunds plus fulfillment cost (concept 09).
**Not covered:** a closing or sales-script method (map row 30). Say so; do not borrow one.

## 3. "New client, no clear offer yet"
**Ask first:** who exactly (business size, owned asset, staffing, goal: the avatar template in concept 02)? Is demand present or are they trying to create it? What do they sell now?
**Read:** concept 02 (great / normal / bad; four indicators) → 05 (five steps: dream outcome, problems, solutions, delivery vehicles, trim and stack) → 03 (price after value) → 04 → 10 (name) → enhancers 06–09 → 21 (money model).
**Flags:** a bad market stops the equation (OF-016); the book gives no thresholds for any market type. Niche duration is not specified (concept 02): do not hard-code. Case multiples such as 5x or 38x are not forecasts (claims s.2).
**Not covered:** how to research a market with data; the books give judgment ratings only.

## 4. "We're competing on price" (commodity offer)
**Ask first:** what do they charge, what does it cost to deliver one more customer (gross profit, OF-007), what do competitors offer?
**Read:** concept 03 (price-to-value; raise price only after raising value) → 04 (which driver can they move, especially time delay and effort) → 05 (trim / stack) → 06–09 (scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees) → 10 (fatigue and naming).
**Flags:** every arrow in the virtuous / vicious price cycle is the author's argument, not tested (concept 03). Scarcity and urgency must be true: real caps and real deadlines (OF-052; LD-012 "make up a reason" conflicts with it). Do not tell a client to raise price without raising delivered or perceived value first.
**Not covered:** how to measure perceived value; how much to raise price.

## 5. "We need a free offer / lead magnet"
**Ask first:** the narrow problem of one avatar; which lead definition counts as success; can they back up every claim in the ad or page?
**Read:** concept 11 (seven steps, lead magnet types and formats) → 22 (attraction offers: the Money Models version of the same job) → 04 (the offer script, LD-020) → 10 (name and test) → 09 (refund-linked offers, if used).
**Flags:** compute cost per customer from stage counts, not LD-013's shorthand. "Free" must show real terms (credit, card on file, conditions, concept 22). The first-ad case (LD-057) says "free" and a cap of "5" yet 19 people paid $299: do not model it as a real cap or a free offer. Test results (poll percentages) are not benchmarks (concept 10).
**Not covered:** the three cold-outreach scripts the Leads copy promises (LD-104).

## 6. "We want to run paid ads"
**Ask first:** is the account live? Who owns it? Is there client data to upload? What is gross profit per customer, and what do they sell after the first purchase? Is 30-day cash covered (see 7)?
**Read:** concept 16 (ad parts, targeting, CTA, landing page, test budget, three phases) → 01 (LTGP, CAC, ratios, payback) → 12 (do paid ads last? time vs money) → 22 and 23 (what makes the ad pay back).
**The base can calculate:** LTGP, CAC, LTGP ÷ CAC; the test-budget rule needs a choice between 30-day **cash** and gross profit (concept 16).
**Flags:** treat as a risk area: look first, show the exact change, wait for the client's yes, note how to undo it. 3:1 is the author's "pattern… not a rule" (concept 01). LTGP:CAC says nothing about payback time. Lookalike audiences built from customer lists are client data: platform terms and privacy law apply, and the book does not discuss either (LD-051). Call-out and claim wording can be treated as misleading by platforms (LD-053).
**Not covered:** platform rules, privacy law, card-network rules.

## 7. Cash is tight, so acquiring customers feels unaffordable
**Ask first:** cost to acquire, cost to deliver, cash collected per new customer in the first 30 days, margin, what they sell after the first sale, how customers pay.
**Read:** concept 01 s.4 (the 30-day test) → 21 (money model, stages, four steps) → 22 → 23 → 24 → 25.
**First decision for the client:** which definition of the 30-day test (what costs count; revenue, cash or profit; which multiple). Nine mentions in Money Models and one in Leads, with no formula (map row 4). A neutral formula is offered in concept 01; the pass mark is the user's.
**Flags:** build one offer at a time, "measure in quarters, not weeks" (MM-067); do not build all four stages at once. The 30-day window is a card-float convenience; card limits, refunds and chargebacks are not discussed. Payments flags to raise when reached: card on file, repeated charge retries, fees disclosed after the card, 4-week billing, a 3% fee, ID retention (concepts 23–25, map s.4). The rule to keep pushing the price up until earnings fall (MM-067) pulls against the 30-day test.
**Not covered:** financing, interest, card limits.

## 8. "Customers leave" / "we want recurring revenue"
**Ask first:** monthly churn (a number from their own data), billing cadence, what customers get after month one, how many leave at the same point.
**Read:** concept 25 (continuity bonus, discount, waived fee) → 17 (referral growth equation uses churn) → 01 (LTGP) → 24 (payment-plan and billing-cadence points).
**Flags:** take retention as an **input**. The 20-month example (MM-053) assumes nobody leaves; with the book's own 10.7% monthly cancellation figure for monthly billing, only about 10% would remain (map s.4). Those 10.7% / 5% / 2% figures are monthly cancellation rates under monthly, quarterly and annual billing, from a third party, unverified (claims s.4); they are not the Gym Launch 10.7% in OF-024. Billing every four weeks, cancellation fees and waived fees need a legal check and honest disclosure (concept 25). In MM-056 the continuity pricing table's "×" applies to the "/mo" figure (standalone ÷ 1.5 in every row), while the text applies it to the first month; which base to use is the user's choice (concept 25; `calc.py standalone --reading`).
**Not covered:** retention methods beyond the offers in concept 25.

## 9. "We've plateaued on one platform / channel"
**Ask first:** current channel and volume; what is stopping them from doing ten times as much (LD-067); estimated returns of the options.
**Read:** concept 12 (More, Better, New; order: more first; new placements → new platforms → new core-four activity) → 20 (constraint, weekly test, volume) → the channel file for the next channel.
**Flags:** the "Size of the Pie" case numbers are framing anecdotes (concept 12). There is no numeric stop rule for when to go "new"; the client estimates returns per option. The seven levels are an order of effort types, not revenue bands.
**Not covered:** forecasting the return of an untried channel.

## 10. "We want to grow beyond our own capacity" (people and partners)
**Ask first:** what is already working personally; budget; who could be a referrer, employee, agency, affiliate?
**Read:** concept 12 (lead-getter order from LD-096 [stated, "what happens naturally"]: referrals → employees → agencies → affiliates; using it as a client sequence is our reading) → 17 → 18 → 19.
**Flags:** referrals are not free if referrers are paid (concept 17). A payout ratio must say which LTGP definition it uses (concept 19). "Pay forever" needs a cap or duration input. Commissions and endorsements may need disclosure (not discussed in the books; see map row 26 and concept 17). Legal limits on cutting employees at three weeks are not covered. An agency is a learning transfer in the book's model, not permanent outsourcing (concept 18).
**Not covered:** employment law, contract terms.

## 11. "Can I use the author's number?"
**Read:** flagged-claims.md. Every number there is an author story, a rule of thumb, an unverified source or a figure that does not tie; none is a benchmark. If the client wants a number, take it from their own business.

## 12. Building a tool or calculator from the base
**Read first:** concept 01, then claims, then the concept file for the topic. Quote the entry ID and the tag for each number.
**Decisions to confirm with the user before coding:**
1. The 30-day test definition (costs / revenue-cash-profit / multiple).
2. LTV or LTGP: gross-profit based, which margin.
3. Which "lead" count.
4. Which "3": LTGP:CAC ≥ 3, or CAC ≤ 3× industry average (concept 01 s.3).
5. Payout ratio definition (concept 19).
6. Rule-of-100 unit (count, minutes, dollars).
7. Cash or gross profit for the ad test budget.
**Never build in:** a 1–10 scale or "max 100" for the Value Equation; weights or a threshold for market indicators; "any missing indicator disqualifies"; any figure from flagged-claims as a default.
**Always build in:** the user's own inputs; relative-comparison labels; division-by-zero guard for the Value Equation; the source ID and tag next to each result; a "not covered by the books" message for closing and for a general sales-script method (Money Models has only scripts inside two offers, MM-019 and MM-047).
**Mark as missing, never invent:** the three cold scripts, the downloadable one-page checklist, the sales book (LD-104).
