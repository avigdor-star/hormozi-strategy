# Concept 24 · Downsell offers (Payment Plan, Trial With Penalty, Feature)

**Books:** Money Models (Section IV, the home). Offers: guarantees and price-to-value. Leads: the sales/ads boundary.
**Built from entries:** MM-042–MM-052.
**Tag key:** [stated] · [figure] · [derived] · [FLAG] · [claim] · [our note].

## What a downsell is
A downsell adjusts the original offer to find the best-value solution that fits the customer's budget; any offer made after a no counts as a downsell. [stated, MM-042]
- Two ways: change **how they pay** (payment plan, trial: same total) or **what they get** (feature downsell: lower price for less).
- Recap definitions (MM-068): **Payment Plan:** same product, same price, with part paid now and the rest spread out over time. **Trial With Penalty:** customers try it free as long as they keep to the terms; if they do not, they pay. **Feature:** lower the price by changing what the customer receives, for example dropping the guarantee takes $400 off.

### The guardrail (MM-042) — the book's six rules (numbering is ours)
1. A no to this offer is not a no to every offer. 2. "Downsells Are Trades": "If you're gonna give something, get something." 3. "Personalize, Don't Pressure." 4. "Offer The Same Things In New Ways" (a hundred ways to offer what you have). 5. **Never lower the price just to land a sale**: that is discounting, not downselling. 6. "Customers Talk About Price": plan prices ahead of time; an in-the-moment cut is, in the author's view, an ethical problem to avoid.
- Boxed story [claim]: a car insurance quote dropped from $5,000 to $400 for the same thing; the buyer lost trust. A different thing can be offered for less; the same thing cannot.
- [our note] The book does discount the same thing in planned offers or trades (attraction offers, rollover credit, the barter in MM-051). Its line is planned offers/trades vs a panic price cut.

## 1. Payment Plan Downsell (MM-043–045)
**Definition:** present the same product again with the cost split, part charged now and the rest in scheduled payments. Why: very often a complaint about price is really a complaint about the amount due at the start. [claim] "tens of millions of dollars."
**Risk:** money can come in one way and go out two ways: more buyers who finish paying (+); cancel before profit (−); would-be pay-in-full customers who take a plan and cancel early (−−).
**Seven steps (up to nine offers; stop when they buy):**
1. **Reward for paying in full** rather than punish for paying over time. Show the plan price (with "interest") then offer a prepay discount: "$15… but it's $10 if you prepay it." [derived] plan is 50% above prepay; prepay discount is 33% off plan. "Same math, but feels better."
2. **3rd-party financing → credit card → layaway** (in that order). Credit card: ask the customer whether they or you should choose the payment terms.
3. **Half now, half later:** "When's the next time you get paid?" Align with paychecks (most paid every two weeks): "boosts 30-day profit way more than monthly payments."
4. **Check they still want it:** ask them to rate from 1 to 10 how much they want to do it. 8 or above → keep offering plans; 7 or below → "Why not a 10?" then a different product (feature downsell). [FLAG] thresholds are the author's rule of thumb.
5. **Three payments** (1/3 now and the next two paychecks, or months).
6. **Evenly spread payments** over the rest of the service (e.g., 16 weekly payments).
7. **Free trial** (concept: Trial With Penalty). "The sale ends here. At least for now."
**Seesaw version (MM-045):** ask whether they prefer large monthly payments or small ones → prepay discount; adjust the down payment until the monthly rate works.
**Built-in upsell:** periodically re-offer the paid-in-full discount during the plan; people offered a slower way to pay will take it, and people given a reason to pay sooner will do that.
**Test that payment plans make money:** close rate should rise; the number/percentage of paid-in-fulls should **not** fall. Example: 10 leads: 3 pay in full; with a downsell 3 more buy on plans → 6 of 10. [FLAG] "number goes down" vs "same percentage" differ if the appointment count changes; the book doesn't say how to know who would have paid in full.
**Billing cadence data [claim, third party "Profitwell," 14,000 businesses, unverified]:** all three are **monthly** cancellation rates, under different billing cadences: 10.7% for monthly billing, 5% for quarterly billing, 2% for annual billing. Begin with fewer, larger payments and move toward smaller ones.
- [FLAG: consumer debt] steering customers to a credit card with a leading choice; check lending and advertising rules.
- **[FLAG: payments]** if the card is declined, try charging it several times that day: repeated charge attempts on a declined card are a payments and cardholder-consent risk; the book gives no guidance. [claim] "recoup a third of my declined payments."
- [FLAG] "Same total price" is inconsistent: interest-loaded plan price vs prepay discount ("a huge discount") vs "keep the overall price the same."

## 2. Trial With Penalty (MM-046–048)
**Definition:** Customers use the product or service free as long as they keep to the agreed terms, and pay only if they break them. Compare Win Your Money Back (get money back if you meet terms). Terms reflect the same actions and results your Win Your Money Back Offer requires; the reward for sticking to the terms is avoiding fees, instead of winning money back. It only changes what they pay **today**, not in total.
**Counting example:** close 3 of 10 on the attraction offer; downsell 4 on a trial; upsell 3 of them → 6 sales ([derived ✓]; assumes 75% of trial takers convert; no measured rate).
**Examples:** B2C "28 Day Kick-That-Habit Blueprint" (attend all calls, post weekly, journal daily, attend sessions); B2B "5-Day Get Your First 5 Customers Challenge" (100 outbound messages/day, daily training, graduation call); software "$500 onboarding then $99/month" (three 60-minute Zoom calls, homework, set up the employer profile) or pay the fee. [FLAG] criteria wording differs from MM-010; the book says to swipe from Win Your Money Back.
**What to give free:** bare-bones offer or the Win Your Money Back offer; "more rather than less—if you can afford it."
**Fee structure:** e.g., $500 product with ten things: **$50 per miss rather than one $500 lump** (author prefers breaking them up; "I've seen both work"). [derived ✓]
**The five steps (figure "HOW I SELL FREE TRIALS"):**
1. **Trial as a downsell, offered last.** Offer to start them free, then ask to take their ID.
2. **Always get a credit card.** Require a card; if the customer hesitates, say that is the usual practice, and if they still refuse, send them away. Anyone who will not leave a card and do the work does not get sold to.
3. **Set the expectation of staying and paying.** Ask: ask whether they would stay long-term if the program delivers the result. If no, "there's no point in giving them a trial." The post-trial price is stated here ("THEN IT'S ONLY $/WK AFTER THAT").
4. **Explain how fees motivate (after getting the card).** Explaining fees before the card is in hand brings more pushback, so explain afterward. Customers initial each fee clause ("to force my sales guys to explain them").
5. **Make sales meetings part of the trial** (3 one-on-one consults; "we charge if you miss those"); the check-ins are "our upsell opportunities."
**Three outcomes (MM-048):** like it (billing is already set up; still offer longer/higher); hate it (ask what to change; "that you're angry at yourself"; offer a higher level; [claim] "about half" buy); didn't use it (reach out multiple times; offer to waive the fee: "A small fee isn't worth a 1-star review").
**Tuning:** nobody takes it → lower requirements/penalties; they take but don't follow through → explain fees, make sales meetings mandatory; they don't stay → emphasize value of staying, deliver better, align back end with front end. Let people make up for goofs. "Just call it a Free Trial."
**Use rules:** Trial With Penalty for recurring products/services where the customer must do work; Pay Less Now (concept 22) for physical products or one-time services. "Not all businesses can do free trials." "Discounts get cards on file" (e.g., first month for $1).
- **[FLAG: transparency, disclosure, payments]** Fees are disclosed after the card on purpose, with "That's just how we've always done it" deflection and "Just call it a Free Trial." This conflicts with Note 6 and Note 5; many places require clear disclosure of fees, billing and cancellation terms before billing information is taken and have rules for trials that convert. Automatic billing after a trial is a negative-option arrangement. **Decide whether to disclose fees before the card. Check local trial, auto-renewal and fee-disclosure rules with counsel.**
- **[FLAG: client data]** "Let me get your ID" / "hold onto the ID": retaining ID raises data-protection questions the book doesn't address.
- [FLAG] "a little fee" vs fees of $50–$500 (state the actual fee). [FLAG: ethics] "angry at yourself" is a scripted emotion; the "LEAVE REVIEW…OR $" criterion is an incentivized-review arrangement. [FLAG] Pay Less Now is an attraction offer in MM-023 and a downsell here.

## 3. Feature Downsell (MM-049–051)
**Definition:** Lower the price by changing what the customer receives: a smaller amount, lower quality, cheaper alternatives, or dropping optional parts. In effect you remove something, cut the price, and ask whether that works now.
**Story [claim]:** a high-ticket seller offered customers a choice between paying less without the money-back option or keeping the money-back guarantee. Before: 100 calls → 25 bought. After: **35 bought the main thing and 40 took the downsell** (75). [derived ✓] Revenue = 35P + 40(P − g) vs 25P before. [FLAG] One business, one quarter; **do not use 25% → 75% as a benchmark**; downsell buyers carry no refund exposure while full-price buyers do.
**Price-to-value rule:** remove stuff they hate and lower the price a lot → better deal (more take the downsell); remove stuff they love and lower the price a little → worse deal (more take the original). The value of what was removed becomes clear once people see the price gap. Remove features **from highest to lowest value.**
**Menu (MM-050):** quantity (fewer sessions, shorter duration); product quality (older versions, lower materials); service quality (time availability, location, cancellations, speed of response/delivery, service ratio one-on-one vs one-to-many vs many-to-one, communication method, provider qualifications, live vs recorded, in-person vs remote, DIY/DWY/DFY, expirations, personalization, insurance/guarantee); remove an entire feature; done-for-you → do-it-yourself (e.g., chiropractor home tools; painter leases a spray machine; "just attend a workshop").
**Rules (MM-051):** "Never negotiate the price" ("business terrorists"): a customer who wants to pay less today gets a payment plan; one who wants to pay less in total gets a feature downsell. "Maintain the position of a helpful guide." Standardize: cut something valuable and lower the price a little first; keep going until they buy. Name the combinations (Whale Package → First Class/Business/Economy; "The Minimum" and "so nothing more than the minimum package then?"). **Temperature check after two downsells:** 1–10; 8 or above → payment-plan downselling; 7 or below → "What would a 10 look like?" Alternate payment-plan and feature downsells ("very difficult to refuse"). After each: "Deal?" / "Fair enough?" Free orientation for DIY downsells [claim] ("about half" showed; "almost all of them bought supplements"). **Downsell your guarantees:** Security is valued, so taking it away makes many people see what it is worth. Feature-downsell **current customers** who don't use a feature, before they cancel [claim] "second highest value of all my customers." **Barter:** $100 off for reviews on all sites, a video testimonial, three social posts, and two friend introductions.
- [FLAG: reviews] Exchanging discounts for reviews/testimonials/posts is an incentivized-review arrangement; platforms and regulators restrict or require disclosure. [FLAG] "Knock $100 off if you…" is the same product for $100 less; the logic is a trade (rule 2). [our note] "Never negotiate" and "trades" are consistent only if every price move is tied to a change in what the customer gets or does.

## Conclusion (MM-052)
"Everybody buys something." Having a hundred offers for one product matters more than having a hundred products with one offer; the offer is never the same goods for less. Free-gift box claims: well-designed payment plans nearly always bring more sales and more money; "Not all businesses can do free trials."

## Do not
- Do not cut the price of the same thing "just because."
- Do not collect a card before disclosing fees, or retain an ID, without a legal and data check for the client.
- Do not retry declined cards on the book's advice without checking card-network rules.
- Do not use 25% → 75%, 10.7%/5%/2%, "about half" as benchmarks.
