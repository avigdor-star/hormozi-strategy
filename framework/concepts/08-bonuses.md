# Concept 08 · Bonuses (the Splinter Stack)

**Books:** Offers (Ch.14). Money Models (Continuity Bonus, gifts inside offers). Leads (CTA step).
**Built from entries:** OF-059, OF-060, OF-061, OF-062, OF-063, OF-064 (and OF-042 for the stack) · LD-055 · MM-054, MM-055 (see concept 25).
**Tag key:** [stated] · [figure] · [derived] · [FLAG] · [claim] · [our note].

## Core idea
Break the offer into components and stack them as bonuses instead of discounting the core. The same offer is worth more when split into its parts and stacked as bonuses than when sold as one. [stated, OF-059]
- Mechanism: **anchor the price to the core offer**, then each bonus **widens the price-to-value discrepancy** until it is "too big to bear."
- Pro tip: **add bonuses instead of discounting the core.** Discounting "teaches your customers that your prices are negotiable."
- [figure] "Splinter Stack": bonus sources are **(A) products, (B) services, (C) next logical need**. C has no visible connector in the image (may be cropped). The name "Splinter Stack" is only in the figure.

## Presenting bonuses one-on-one (OF-060)
1. **Ask for the sale first.** If yes, reveal the bonuses afterward.
2. If no: present **one bonus matching their perceived obstacle**, then ask again ("Fair enough?"); repeat with another bonus if needed.
- The text admits the bonuses were going to be provided anyway; layering "increases the perception" of value. Rationale: "People have a hard time rejecting reciprocity"; they "feel almost obligated to buy."
- [our note] The "obligated" mechanism is persuasion pressure; keep for the ethics review. Sequence a tool can use: ask → address the stated obstacle with the matching bonus → ask again.

## Bonus checklist: the author's 11 rules (OF-061)
1. Always offer bonuses (use the bundle from the Stack step).
2. Give each a **special name with a benefit in the title**.
3. Tell them (a) how it relates to their issue, (b) what it is, (c) how you discovered/created it, (d) how it improves their life (faster, easier, less effort and sacrifice).
4. Provide **proof** (stat, past client, personal experience).
5. Paint a **vivid mental image** of life after using it.
6. Always **ascribe a price tag** and justify it.
7. **Tools and checklists beat additional trainings** (lower effort and time, so higher value).
8. Each should address a **specific concern or obstacle** about why they can't or won't succeed.
9. Can be what they will realistically need **next**.
10. The value of the bonuses should **exceed** the value of the core offer (widens the gap, implies the core is worth more).
11. Add **scarcity and urgency to the bonuses themselves**.

## Scarcity vs urgency on bonuses (OF-062)
- Scarcity (not time-bound): only sign-ups get Bonus 1, 2, 3, never sold elsewhere; or only 3 tickets remain for a $5,000 virtual event, and buying the program gets you one of those last three.
- Urgency (time-bound): buy today and I add [bonus], which normally costs $1,000, at no charge.

## Bonuses from other businesses (OF-063)
Method: get non-competing businesses to give services/products as your bonuses in return for **free exposure** to your clients (you get high-value bonuses at no cost and "referral IOUs").
$400 pain-clinic offer [stated]: chiropractor 2 adjustments $100 · food company discounts $50 · braces/orthotics discounts $150 · health club session + month $100 · pharmacist discounts $100/mo.
- [derived] Sum = **$500** > $400 ✓. [FLAG] the pharmacist's "$100/mo savings" is treated as a one-time $100.
- Commission add-on [stated]: chiro $100/person; food company free food; orthotics $100/person; health club free membership OR $50/person; pharmacy $100/person; claim: "possibility of making us an extra **$350**."
- [derived] $100 + $100 + $50 + $100 = **$350** only if the club commission is $50, the free food counts as $0, **and** the same customer uses every partner. [FLAG] The book calls it "pure profit" without those conditions.
- Prestige Labs example [claim]: muddled text; read as gym owners' customers get **30% off**; the sponsored athlete is paid **40% of sales netted after the discount**.

## What belongs in bonuses (OF-064)
- Good assets: checklists, tools, swipe files, scripts, templates, recorded workshops/webinars/events/interviews, information products (high perceived value, low cost, "zero operational effort besides giving an additional login"), tickets to virtual events, a month of VIP service (also an upsell path).
- Rule: anything you create once at a real cost but can give away endlessly fits a bonus. Keep a "vault" of bonus assets for closing.
- Core vs bonus: use **"wow factor"**: pull out distinct, short, high-value pieces that "can almost stand on their own."

## Cross-book
- **Money Models Continuity Bonus** (MM-054/055): a great extra for signing up today, which is usually worth more than the first continuity payment. See concept 25.
- **Leads** LD-055: add urgency, scarcity and bonuses to the CTA.
- The Offers book's "Book II" reference for VIP service as an upsell is ambiguous (OF-064, OF-081); Money Models is our reading.

## Do not
- Do not present a bonus as new value if it was always included without saying so in the user's own words; the book itself admits this (OF-060). Keep the honesty decision with the user.
- Do not use $350 or $500 as expected outcomes.
