# Concept 11 · Lead magnets and engaged leads

**Books:** Leads (Section II, the home). Offers supplies the standard a free offer must meet. Money Models treats the same job as "attraction offers" (concept 22).
**Built from entries:** LD-002, LD-004, LD-005, LD-006, LD-007, LD-008, LD-009, LD-010, LD-011, LD-012, LD-013, LD-014 (also LD-001, LD-003, LD-015).
**Tag key:** [stated] · [figure] · [derived] · [FLAG] · [claim] · [our note].

## Definitions (LD-002, LD-004)
- **Lead** = "a person you can contact. That's all." **Engaged lead** = a lead who **shows interest** (gives contact info on a website; follows and is contactable; replies to an email campaign). "Engaged leads are the true output of advertising." [stated]
- [FLAG] Section I (p.9) uses a different definition: a lead has a problem to solve and money to spend. **Three different counts:** contactable (p.41) · engaged (p.41–42) · has problem and money (p.9). A tool must store which one a number refers to.
- **Offer** = what you promise to give in exchange for something of value. **Core offer** = your product/service for money; advertising it goes "straight for the sale." Going straight for the sale might be all you need, so try that first. **Lead magnet** = "a complete solution to a narrow problem," typically lower-cost or free, that **reveals another problem** your core offer solves. [stated]
- When to use a lead magnet: when people want to know more before buying; "common for businesses that sell more expensive stuff." Test: valuable enough that you **could charge for it**. Someone who spends time now is more likely to spend money later. [stated]
- Grand Slam standard applies to free offers: the Grand Slam Offer standard works at least as well for free offers as for paid ones. [stated]

## Scope (LD-003, LD-001)
- The book ends at a **"raised hand"**: strangers show interest. It does **not** cover closing the sale. It assumes you already have a Grand Slam Offer.
- Two ways to grow: get more customers; make them worth more (this book = the first). More customers = more, better, cheaper, **reliable** leads. Doubling your leads doubles your business (all else equal). [stated] No metric is attached to better/cheaper/reliable. [our note]
- [FLAG] The claim that advertising lets you make money even with a terrible product [claim] conflicts with Offers' warning that guarantees on a poor product backfire (OF-065). Don't use it as a rule.

## The seven steps (LD-005)
1. Figure out the problem and who to solve it for · 2. Figure out how to solve it · 3. Figure out how to deliver it · 4. **Test** what to name it · 5. Make it easy to consume · 6. Make it darn good · 7. Make it easy for them to tell you they want more.
[FLAG] Steps 4 and 7 are worded differently in the opening list and the recap; the body text is the authority.

**Step 1 · Problem-Solution cycle (LD-006, figure):** Each problem can be solved, and each solution uncovers further problems. Pick a problem that is **narrow and meaningful**; solve it (free); that reveals a **new** problem; **if your core offer solves that new problem, you've got a winner.** Example: home-selling (value, staging, repairs, etc.) → free solution to one; the remaining problem is the paid core offer.

**Step 2 · Three types (LD-007):**
| Type | What it does | Works best when |
|---|---|---|
| Reveal their problem (diagnosis) | Makes the lead aware of a problem they didn't know | The problem gets worse the longer you wait |
| Samples and trials | Full but brief access to the core offer (limit uses, time, or both) | Core offer is a recurring solution to a recurring problem |
| One step of a multi-step process | One valuable step free, the rest when they buy | Core offer solves a more complex problem |

**Step 3 · Four delivery formats (LD-008):** software · information · services · physical products. Grid: 3 types × 4 formats = **12** lead magnets for one narrow problem. [derived ✓] Make many versions and **rotate**. Do not merge with the Offers Delivery Cube (concept 05).

**Step 4 · Test the name (LD-009):** test **headline, image, subheadline**; headline first. Cheap polls/replies. See concept 10 for the poll data and flags. Claims: "2x, 3x, or 10x your engagement" [claim].

**Step 5 · Easy to consume (LD-010):** Take-rate increases of 2x, 3x, or even 4x and more [claim, no source]. By format: software = phone and computer; information = every format; services = more times/days/ways to reach you; physical = easy to order, fast delivery. This is Effort & Sacrifice applied to the free offer (concept 04).

**Step 6 · Give away the secrets (LD-011):** provide **more value than the cost of your core offer before they've bought it.** There is no such thing as giving too much value, only too little. [stated] 99% of people will not buy, but they will build or wreck your reputation. [claim]

**Step 7 · The CTA (LD-012):** CTA = **(1) what to do** + **(2) reasons to do it right now.** "Not 'don't delay' but instead 'call now.'" Three reasons: scarcity (reality test: whether you could cope if tomorrow brought 1000x as many customers; trade-off "the fewer you have, the fewer engaged leads"), urgency, and "Fraternity Party Planner" (make up a reason). See concepts 06 and 07.
- [FLAG: ethics] "Make up a reason" / "any reason (even bad ones)" can be read as permitting false reasons; require **true** reasons. The Harvard line-cutting study is unnamed [claim].

## Worked example: cost per customer with vs without a lead magnet (LD-013)
Rule [stated]: a lead magnet that costs money to deliver should still lower the cost of getting a new customer. Assumed profit on core offer: **$10,000**.
| | Core offer advertised | Lead magnet advertised |
|---|---|---|
| Ad cost to get a person on a call | $1,000 | $75 + $25 to deliver = $100 |
| Conversion | 1 in 3 calls closes | 1 in 10 who get the magnet buys |
| Cost per customer | $3,000 | $1,000 |
| Return on $10,000 profit | 3.3 : 1 [derived] | **10 : 1** |
[derived] 3,000 ÷ 1,000 = 3x cheaper ✓; 10,000 ÷ 1,000 = 10:1 ✓.
- [FLAG] Unit problem 1: "$100 per call" is later multiplied by 10 as if it were cost per **magnet recipient**; $75 + $25 is the cost per engaged lead, not per booked call. Unit problem 2: the two funnels compare different stages (call → customer vs. recipient → customer). **A tool must compute cost per customer from explicit stage counts (ad spend → engaged leads → buyers), not from the shorthand.**
- [our note] $25 delivery cost is counted for the magnet only.

## What a good lead magnet does (LD-014)
1. Engages ideal customers when they see it. 2. Gets more people to engage than the core offer alone. 3. Is valuable enough that they consume it. 4. Makes the right people more likely to buy.

## Claims not to use as benchmarks (LD-015)
$250M+ annual revenue; 20,000+ leads/day; 36:1 lifetime ad return; origin-story revenue; Acquisition.com averages (1.8x revenue, 3.01x profit in 12 months) with no baseline. Case-study ad story: a webinar produced 80 leads and 0 sales over $450 of ads; a ~13-minute case study (a case study headlined with 213 new members and $112,000 of revenue) filled the calendar overnight. One case, no control.
