# Concept 13 · Warm outreach (Core Four #1)

**Books:** Leads (Section III, the home). Offers supplies the offer script (Value Equation) and the "generous first offer."
**Built from entries:** LD-016, LD-017, LD-018, LD-019, LD-020, LD-021, LD-022, LD-023, LD-024.
**Tag key:** [stated] · [figure] · [derived] · [FLAG] · [claim] · [our note].

## What it is
One-to-one contact with people who gave you permission to contact them (friends, family, followers, current and previous customers, contacts). It is the cheapest, easiest route to people interested in what you sell, yet most businesses skip it. Works with 100 or 1,000,000 contacts. Best for the **first five clients** of a new product; for advanced users, re-engagement and new product lines. [stated, LD-017]
- Character: few engaged leads per hour at first (everything personal), "but, for that reason, it is reliable."
- Two limits [stated]: **time** (at the start, getting customers should take most of your time, at least four hours per day, as the first thing you do after getting up); **audience size** (you eventually "run out"; add the second core-four activity, posting content: concept 14).
- [our note: compliance] Warm = "gave you permission." The book does not discuss anti-spam/telemarketing consent. **Having someone's number is not consent.** A tool must not assume otherwise.

## Where it sits in the Core Four (LD-016)
| | Warm (know you) | Cold (don't) |
|---|---|---|
| 1-to-1 private | **Warm outreach** | Cold outreach |
| 1-to-many public | Post free content | Run paid ads |
Automation doesn't change the nature: emailing a 10,000-person list is "one-to-one really fast by a machine." Claim: when leads fall short of what you want, the core four are not being done with enough skill or volume. No numeric test for either. See concept 12.

## A-C-A reply (LD-019)
**Acknowledge** what they said → **Compliment** (tie to a positive trait) → **Ask** a question leading toward your offer. Example: "Two kids. And you're an accountant…" → "Wow! Supermom!…" → "Do you get time for yourself?" [our note] Do not auto-generate compliments the user can't mean.

## Making the offer (LD-020)
Uses the Value Equation (concept 04) as a message checklist. Short form: a one-line statement that you help (ideal customer) reach (dream outcome) within (time period) without (effort and sacrifice), plus (increased perceived likelihood of achievement). Full form asks whether they know anybody who is…, and adds that you are taking on **five free case studies** because that is all you can manage. Design: they are **not asked to buy**, only whether they know anyone; "In all three outcomes, you win" (interest, referral, or both).
- Limit: stay as near to it as you can without lying or overstating.
- [FLAG] Saying that a named person has just worked with you is honest only if the result exists; no invented testimonials. "Five… that's all I can handle" must be a real cap.
- Image-only Pro Tip: 11 ways to raise perceived likelihood (listed in concept 04).

## First five free (LD-021)
- Make the first five free; the precise count is less important than understanding why it helps you. Four reasons: reps making offers; "you probably suck (for now)" and free is forgiven; guinea pigs to improve; free customers leave reviews, give feedback, send friends. Terms up front: use it, give feedback, leave a killer review if deserved.
- If people won't take it free, "your 'free' stuff is too expensive": they don't want it, don't believe you, or hidden costs (time, effort) are too high. Ask: **what would I need to change to make continuing worthwhile for you?** "Yeses give me opportunity. Nos give me feedback."
- Links to Offers "make the first offer generous" (OF-043).

## Charging: the schedule (LD-022)
Trigger: "Once people start referring, start charging." Swap "free" for "**80% off for the next five**," then "**60% off**," then "**40% off**," then raise the price by 20% after every five, until you find your best price. Adds urgency because prices actually go up.
[FLAG] The book stops at 40% off and then says "raise it by 20% every five." Not stated whether 20 percentage points of list (consistent with 80/60/40) or 20% above the last price. **Ask the user to choose.** Links to Offers price-increase announcement (OF-057, concept 07).

## Keeping the list warm (LD-023)
Give regular value; **9-word email** (Dean Jackson): "Are you still looking to **[4 word desire]**?" No images, no links, just a question. Replies = engaged leads. [derived] 5 + 4 = 9 words ✓.

## Benchmarks and money math (LD-024)
Funnel [stated; figure]: **about 1 in 5** contacts engage (100 reach-outs ≈ 20 replies) → about **1 in 5-ish** of repliers take the free offer (≈ **4**) → convert **1** later. **1 customer per 100 reach-outs.** Figure: "1–2% will buy." These rates are "about" values that shift depending on how valuable your offer is and how strongly they trust you. [claim] Not thresholds.
Money math: 1% of the list buys a $400 offer; 500 reach-outs/week = 5 customers/week; 5 × $400 = $2,000/week; × 52 = **$104,000**. [derived ✓] Author's comparison: "still two times the median household income" [claim].
- [FLAG] It needs **500 reach-outs/week for 52 weeks = 26,000 reach-outs** but a 1,000-contact list is "ten solid days" at 100/day. The math silently assumes tens of thousands of contacts or re-contacting. **A tool must carry list size as an explicit limit.**
- [FLAG] 500/week assumes 5 working days at 100/day; the $400 price ignores the free/discount phases the book recommends first.
- Daily checklist (figure): who = yourself; what = first five free; where = phone/email/mail/SMS; when = first four hours; how much = 100 attempts/day; follow up twice; until you get customers.
- Origin story [claim]: first business, free 12-week training for charity donation, six said yes, referrals brought 5–6 more, about **$4,000/month**.
