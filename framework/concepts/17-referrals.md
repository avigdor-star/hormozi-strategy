## Lead getters and leverage (LD-069)
- Leverage = the amount obtained relative to the time spent obtaining it. **Lead getters** = people other than you who let others know about your stuff using the core four. [stated]
| Scenario | Work | Leads | Leverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 You are the lead getter | HIGH | LOW | LOW |
| 2 One lead getter does the core four for you | LOW | LOW | HIGH |
| 3 Lots of lead getters (your time spent recruiting) | HIGH | HIGH | HIGHER |
| 4 A lead getter who gets lead getters | LOW | HIGH | HIGHEST |
- You don't "do" affiliates, referrals, agencies or employees as a core-four method; you use the core four to **get** them, so "the core four stacks." "Ideally, every lead becomes a lead getter."
- Four types: **customers** (referrals), **employees**, **agencies**, **affiliates** (concepts 17, 18, 19). [our note] The table is qualitative; no leverage formula beyond the one-line definition.

## What a referral is and why it matters (LD-070)
- A **referrer** sends an engaged lead to your business; best from customers. Two benefits: **higher LTGP** ("buy more expensive stuff… pay in cash upfront" [claim, no data]); **lower CAC** ("costs you nothing").
- Example [stated]: LTGP:CAC = 4:1 → CAC = 25% of LTGP; if **every customer brings two more**, ratio = **12:1** (CAC ≈ 8.3% of LTGP). [derived ✓]
- Core-four outputs are roughly linear in inputs; word of mouth is exponential.
- **Referral growth equation** [figure]: **% clients referred monthly − % clients churned monthly = % monthly compounding growth.** Referrals > churn → grow without other advertising; = churn → need other advertising to grow; < churn → "you've got to advertise to break even."
- [FLAG] "Costs you nothing" and 12:1 assume free referrals; the book later teaches paying referrers your full CAC (LD-074 forms 1–2), so then the benefit is quality and LTGP, not lower CAC. 12:1 also assumes two referrals per customer. **Use the growth equation (rates), not the example, in a calculator.**
- [our note] The equation measures net growth from referrals only; other acquisition is outside it.

## Why most businesses don't get referrals; goodwill (LD-071)
- Two reasons: the product isn't as good as the owner thinks; they don't ask. Test question: why do my customers feel too embarrassed to bring up my product with the people around them?
- **Goodwill** [stated]: you charge a price, the customer receives value, and the difference between them is goodwill. Figure: stack Value (top) > Price > Cost; band value−price = **goodwill**, price−cost = **profit**. Lowering price is "at best, a temporary solution." The question is "how do we give more value?"
- [our note] Raising price at constant value shrinks goodwill; the book doesn't say how to balance beyond "give more value." Same idea as Offers' price-to-value (concept 03).

## Six ways to give more value (LD-072)
| # | Ad part | Lever | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call outs | Sell better customers | Find what lowest-churn customers share; target them. [claim] PR firm: best niche = **15%** of business; feared losing **85%**; changed call outs; referrals "like clockwork." |
| 2 | Dream outcome | Set better expectations | Gradually lower the promises you make, until your close rates drop; at that point, stop. |
| 3 | Perceived likelihood | Get more people better results | Six steps: survey best results → interview → list common actions → **force new customers to repeat those actions** → measure improvement → **match your guarantee's conditions** to those actions. [claim] Owners who ran paid ads and made a sale in the first 7 days had **triple the LTGP**. |
| 4 | Time delay | Make faster wins | Five tactics: small things at shorter intervals; updates are wins; many wins in the first **48 hours** [claim]; **BAMFAM** (Book-A-Meeting-From-A-Meeting); deliver early, **add fifty percent to timelines**. |
| 5 | Effort & sacrifice | Keep making your stuff better | Six-step monthly loop: most common problem → fix from successful customers → improve → small group of strugglers → feedback → next problem. |
| 6 | Call to action | Tell them what to buy next | If the product is great they'll want more; sell again. Most people fixate on front-end offers and overlook the back end. |
- [FLAG] Lever 2 (lower promises) vs Offers (raise dream outcome and likelihood): treat as optimization with a close-rate floor; the true promise must stay honest.
- [FLAG] Lever 3 step 4: the 7-day finding is a correlation; the book doesn't test whether forcing the actions causes the gain.
- Cross-book: levers 2–5 apply the Value Equation (concept 04); lever 3 step 6 ties to guarantee conditions (concept 09); lever 6 is upsell territory (concept 23).

## "One Question to Rule Them All" (LD-073)
Thought experiment: you lost all customers but one; no core four; all future customers must come from this one customer. How would you treat them, what results would they need, what onboarding, what type of customer? "Write it out… Then do it." [our note] A design prompt for the six levers, not a measurable test.

## Asking: the seven ways (LD-074)
Rule: referral requests work only when treated as an offer. Three components of a program: how you give the incentive, what you incentivize with, how you ask. [claim] Dropbox "39 x'd their business in fifteen months"; PayPal $10 credit each side, a million users in two years.
| # | Way | Rule and numbers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One-sided benefit | Pay your average CAC to the referrer **or** the friend. E.g., CAC $200: a real **three-way introduction** at purchase; $200 check to referrer **or** $200 off to the friend. Ask for the spouse; household discount. |
| 2 | Two-sided benefits | Split CAC: half to referrer, half to friend. E.g., $500 program, CAC $200: **$100 cash** + **$100 off**, up to **3 friends**. [derived ✓] |
| 3 | Ask right when they buy | On the contract/checkout: names and numbers. People who join the program with another person tend to achieve 3x the results (script text; the [claim] in LD-074 is the salesperson anecdote that follows). |
| 4 | Referral as negotiation chip | Trade a lower price for introductions (e.g., $500 price, $400 offer for three friends). Charging different prices for the same product is ethically fine when the terms differ. |
| 5 | Referral events | Points/credits/dollars for bringing friends in **1 to 4 weeks** ("Bring-a-friend," "Spouse Challenge"). |
| 6 | Ongoing programs | Talk about doing things with others all the time. [claim] A buddy saw a **33%** boost; 250,000 of 1,000,000 ticket buyers referred ([derived] 25%; not stated in the book). |
| 7 | Unlockable bonuses | Bonuses for those who (1) refer and (2) leave a testimonial; cheaper than cash. |
- Combined promotion: give everyone a gift card worth **one-third of the program cost**, expiring in **7–14 days**; the referrer says they got a $2000 gift card and asks if the friend wants it [derived: implies a $6,000 program]. PS: sell the gift cards at **90% off** (the book does not say whether that means a price 90% below face value, e.g. about $200 for a $2,000 card).
- [FLAG: compliance/honesty] Paying referrers or rewarding testimonials can trigger endorsement-disclosure rules (not mentioned). The 90%-off gift card "looks like they spent a lot of money" framing should not mislead buyers about value. Form 4 charges different prices for the same product (fairness/pricing rules not discussed). Check for client work.
- [FLAG] "Seven combos" are examples of combinations of three components, **not seven independent options.**

## Conclusion and baseline (LD-075)
Referring is a **risk** for the customer (they risk goodwill with a friend); they refer when the benefit outweighs that risk: add incentives and lower the risk by delivering. Action: find your referral percentage and your churn percentage to use as a baseline. [claim] Case: ads off for two weeks; weekly closes fell **21 → 15** yet "$500,000 a week from word of mouth"; $30M of customers cite a referral. [our note] The $42,000 here is an event price; Offers' $42,000 was a yearly agreement price (OF-024). Not benchmarks.
Roadmap link: Level 4 asks for **25% or more** of customers from referrals; the machine says "a third" (concept 12).

## Do not
- Do not model referrals as free if you also pay referrers.
- Do not present the 90%-off gift card as a real discount value to a buyer who believes otherwise.
- Do not use 12:1, 33%, 25%, 3x as expected results.
