## What it is
One-to-many advertising to cold audiences; "renting eyeballs or earballs." "You trade money for reach." Reach can be bought with certainty; a return on the spend cannot, so the game is efficiency, not reach. Ads fail mostly because "the right people never saw it." [stated, LD-049]
- Method: start with the whole world and narrow to the engaged: (1) pick a platform with your audience; (2) use targeting; (3) craft the ad to **repel everyone else**; (4) tell whoever is left to take the next step.
- Scaling: profit in a small puddle, then pond, lake, ocean. As the audience grows, efficiency falls but total profit rises. [derived ✓] $1,000 → $10,000 = 10:1, $9,000 profit; $100,000 → $300,000 = 3:1, $200,000 profit. Rule: grow the audience as large as you can while it still makes a profit. [our note] That is the only stop rule; no minimum ratio.
- Order in the Core Four: paid ads are the fastest way to scale leads but go **last** (skills transfer from the other three; they cost money you'll have). [FLAG] The author's own history put paid ads early (LD-068); not reconciled.

## Part I: making the ad (four problems, four steps)
**Step 1 · Platform (LD-050):** all four true: you've used it as a consumer; you can target your people; you know how to format ads for it; you have the minimum money.
**Step 2 · Target (LD-051):** two methods, separately or together:
| Method | Rule |
|---|---|
| Lookalike audience | upload a list (platform minimum size); bigger/higher-quality list = more responsive |
| Factors of your choosing | age, income, gender, interests, time, location (exclude who never buys) |
List order for the lookalike: current/previous customers → warm reach-out list → cold reach-out leads → later paid-ad leads who didn't buy. More filters = more efficient ads but you burn the audience faster; "This is how you scale." If you can't make a lookalike, "start by targeting interests."
[our note] Customer lists are client data: platform terms and privacy law apply to uploading; the book doesn't discuss either. [derived] The book's 99%/99.5% "excluded" numbers are rounded illustrations, not calculations.
**Step 3 · The ad has three parts (LD-052):** (1) **Call out** (get noticed) (2) **Value** (get interested) (3) **CTA**. "The headline is the first sale." [stated] Ads "became 20x more effective" after focusing on the first five seconds (one before/after).
- **Call outs (LD-053):** verbal: **labels** ("Clark County Moms"; local ladder Americans < Texans < Dallas Residents < Irving Residents), **yes-questions**, **if-then statements**, **ridiculous results**; nonverbal: **contrast**, **likeness** (people like your audience; mascots), **the scene**.
- [FLAG] "Attractive people… convert better" is a [claim]. Patterns like "class action lawsuit" or "The government is handing out thousand dollar checks" are treated as misleading by platforms/regulators unless true. **Every call-out claim must be true and platform-compliant.**
- **Get them interested: What × Who × When (LD-054, figure).** Eight elements (more: Dream, Fast, Easy, Likely; less: Nightmare, Slow, Hard, Risky), seen from many people's perspectives, across past, present, future. See concept 04 for the flags (Speed/Ease pairing, 4 vs 8 variables, 120 angles). Long and short ads differ only in how many angles there is time to cover. [FLAG] The promised end-of-chapter tips are **not in the book**. [FLAG: ethics] "Nightmare" is a fear appeal; true, supportable consequences only.
- **CTA (LD-055):** "S-P-E-L-L it out." Make it quick and easy (short URLs). Add urgency, scarcity and bonuses (concepts 06–08).
**Step 4 · Get permission to contact (LD-056, figure):** "Get. Their. Contact. Information." Simple landing page; three layouts (1: headline, sub-head, email, submit; 2: + image; 3: + image + bullets); match the ad's look and language ("click to close"); consistency technique ("Now that you just did A…"). No landing-page conversion rate is given here.

**The chain (LD-058):** right people see it → notice (call outs) → consume and get a reason to act now (value) → give permission (landing page) = engaged lead. [our note] Diagnostic order: reach → notice → interest → landing-page conversion.

### First-ad case (LD-057, [claim])
Ad: a local ad seeking 5 Chino Hills residents for a free 6-week challenge. $1,000 test; **19 people at $299** = $5,681 ([derived] ✓, assuming "just under $5700" means revenue); the owner's check $2,500 (> half the $4,681 profit = $2,340). "At least $1.5B in revenue" afterwards. [FLAG] "FREE," a cap of "5," and 19 sales at $299 are not reconciled; **do not model "5" as a cap or "free" as the price**; an ad's promise should match what is sold. Single case, local, 2013; not an expectation.

## Part II: the money
### Three phases of scaling (LD-059)
| Phase | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 Track money | Set up tracking before spending a dollar |
| 2 Lose money | You pay first; expect to lose more often than you win; make losses small, wins big |
| 3 Print money | If returns exceed spend, "spend as much as you can" within what you can handle; reverse the budget from the sales goal |
- Illustration: $100 on each of ten ads = $1,000; nine lose, one returns $500 (5:1); "100x down": $10,000 → $50,000. [derived ✓]
- **Test-budget rule:** budget **2x the cash collected from a customer in the first 30 days** (not LTGP); example $100 → let an ad spend up to **$200** if it's getting leads; **no leads at all → shut off before 1x ($100)**.
- [FLAG: cash vs profit] The rule says "cash I collect… (not LTGP)" but the example says "$100 in **profit**." Pick one and say which. No rule for how many leads count as "getting leads."
- **Phase 3 budget:** customers wanted × CAC, **padded 20%**; 100 × $100 = $10,000 → $12,000 over 30 days = **$400/day**. [derived ✓] The 20% pad is the author's habit, not a fixed rule.
- [claim] A business took a year to get ads profitable. "Measure the returns over a long time horizon."

### LTGP, CAC, and 3:1 (LD-060, figure)
- **LTGP** = all the money a customer spends minus all the money it takes to deliver it. Example: $15 sale, $5 delivery → $10 gross profit; ten purchases → **LTGP $100**. **CAC** = cost to acquire a customer. **Efficiency = LTGP ÷ CAC.** Gross profit, not revenue, is what funds getting customers, paying rent and covering payroll. (Same as OF-008.)
- LTGP > CAC = profitable; < CAC = losing; below 3 to 1 it is hard to scale; above 3 to 1 growth accelerates; the author says this is a pattern he personally observed, not a rule.
- Two levers: lower CAC or raise LTGP ("both").
- **Diagnosis rule:** CAC **below 3x the industry average** → focus on business model (LTGP); **above 3x** → focus on advertising (CAC) (LD-060; LD-080 words it differently: "a sales problem or an advertising problem," see concept 18).
- [FLAG] The number 3 means two different things: LTGP:CAC ≥ 3 (target) and CAC ≤ 3× industry average (diagnosis). Keep as two checks; the line is a heuristic. LTGP:CAC says nothing about payback time.

### Client-financed acquisition: the 30-day cash rule (LD-061, figure)
- Problem: LTGP > CAC but first-purchase profit is below CAC, so cash arrives slowly and "cripples your ability to scale."
- Rule: if the customer spends more than it costs to **get and fulfill** them **in the first 30 days**, you can scale "now and forever." Why 30 days: any business can borrow interest-free for thirty days using a credit card.
- Example [stated]: $15/mo, $5 delivery → $10 GP/month; 10-month stay → LTGP $100; CAC $30 → **3.3:1** ✓. Old way [figure]: month 0 cash −$20, −$10 at month 1, **break even at month 2**, +$40 by month 6. Fix: a **$100 upsell with 100% margin** that **one in five** take → $20 average → first-30-day GP = $30 = CAC. New way [figure]: month 0 cash $0, +$60 by month 6.
- [FLAG] Assumes a **100% margin** and **20% take rate** inside 30 days; at 50% margin the upsell adds $10 and break-even fails. **Margin and take rate must be explicit inputs.**
- [our note: risk] The 30-day window is a card-float convenience; the book doesn't discuss card limits, interest after 30 days, refunds or chargebacks (refunds cost $150,000 in the origin story, LD-015). **Live ad accounts and payments are a risk area.**
- Cross-book: Money Models' 30-day rule appears at MM-004, MM-064, MM-068, with no single definition (see concept 01).

### Lessons and checklist (LD-062, LD-063)
- **Sales problem vs ad problem:** CAC includes non-ad costs (sales). [claim] A company spent 12 weeks and $150,000 on ads; "the ads worked fine… their sales sucked." Test [stated]: if engaged leads have the issue your offer solves and can afford it yet still do not buy, the ads are fine and the trouble lies in sales.
- Best free content can make the best paid ads (claim: "nine times out of ten"); user-generated content "often" makes killer ads.
- The author says he can teach ad placement in twenty minutes, for $100. Start with a sum you can accept losing every month.
- Checklist [figure]: how much = learning budget, then reverse to sales goal; how many = "30+ call outs × 10 ads" (unexplained; our guess: 30+ call-out variations across 10 ads).

## Do not
- Do not use 2x, 20%, 3:1, 3x industry average, 20% take rate as universal rules.
- Do not call something "profitable ads" from LTGP:CAC without a payback-time check.
- Do not build a calculator for the test budget without choosing cash or gross profit.
