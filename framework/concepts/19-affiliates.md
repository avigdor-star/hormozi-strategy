## Definition and why (LD-085, LD-086)
- An affiliate is a business of its own that urges its audience to buy from you. Unlike referrals: they have their own business and advertising, and agree to offer your stuff to their engaged leads **in exchange for money, free stuff, or both**. You get them by advertising to them with an offer, like a customer; but "affiliates demand a unique type of offer": a fast, simple way to make commissions. [stated]
- Leverage math [stated]: 10 customers/month at $10,000 = $1.2M in 12 months; vs. 10 affiliates/month each bringing one $10,000 customer per month = **$7.8M** ([derived ✓] $100,000 × 78).
- ALAN [claim]: super-affiliate → agencies → local businesses → consumer leads; "$1,700,000 per month within six months."
- [FLAG] Both examples assume **zero affiliate churn and constant output.** The book later says affiliates must be activated and kept active. Do not use $7.8M as a forecast.
- Track record [claim]: ALAN and Prestige Labs: "$75,000,000 in revenue from over 5000+ affiliates." [our note] Signed vs active are different measures (Prestige Labs "400+ active").
- **Six steps:** find · offer · qualify · pay · launch · integrate.

## Step 1 · Find ideal affiliates (LD-087)
The ideal affiliate has a business whose warm audience is full of people similar to your customers. Ask about your best customers: what they buy (who provides it), where they go, what they like to do; if direct to consumer: **the employers of your consumers.** The author listed **200 products and services** and found categories: software, products, equipment, services, groups they belong to, events they attended. Multiple-category businesses likely have more leads. Make a sheet; if stuck, call your customers.

## Step 2 · The affiliate offer (LD-088)
Call out + value + CTA like any offer. Value template: dream outcome (more money from current customers) · perceived likelihood (their customers already want the product) · effort & sacrifice (no building/delivering/support) · time delay (sell it tomorrow). "Make affiliates the customer you're advertising to." (Concept 04.)

## Step 3 · Qualify (LD-089)
- Affiliates become real when they understand and agree to terms; in nine cases out of ten, paying affiliates will give it attention. [claim]
- Way 1: **make them a customer** (buy/use the product). Way 2: **make them an expert** (pay for training that certifies them; purchase can be credit toward certification). Author's favorite: both.
- **Pricing rule:** charge **10–20% of what the average active affiliate makes in the first twelve months** (e.g., $40,000/year → $4,000–$8,000). the author found that 10–20% gets the most people to become active affiliates. [claim] If starting with physical products, bulk purchasing; or raise the minimum investment every 5 sign-ups. if too few people start, lower the commitment; if too few follow through, raise it.
- [our note] The rule needs an estimate of average earnings, which a new program lacks. **Charging affiliates for training/certification is a business-opportunity-style structure some jurisdictions regulate; check local rules.**

## Step 4 · What to pay (LD-090)
- Pay for a defined action: new customers, repeat customers; later pre-purchase steps (lead magnets, appointments). Pay based on **maximum allowable CAC**.
- Worked example [stated]: product $200, fulfillment $40 → $160 left. Target 3:1 → three parts for the business ($120) and one part ($40) for the affiliate → **maximum allowable CAC = $40**.
- **Three-tier payout** (percent of the $40): 1: **25% = $10** on agreeing to terms · 2: **50% = $20** on activating · 3: **100% = $40** on sustaining performance (e.g., five customers/month).
- Blended example: 20% of sales from tier 1, 20% tier 2, 60% tier 3 → **$30** ([derived ✓] 2 + 4 + 24) → "improved from 3:1 to 4:1."
- [FLAG] Ratio language: by the book's own LTGP definition ($160 gross profit), CAC $40 gives **4:1**, not 3:1; "3:1" treats LTGP as the $120 after paying the affiliate. "Improved to 4:1" divides $120 by $30; counting the $10 saved the share is $130 → 4.33:1. **Use one definition per calculation; take gross profit per customer and payout per customer as separate inputs.**
- [FLAG] $40 → $30 is a **25%** cut, not 33%. The idea that a 33% cut in marketing costs can raise net profit by 10% to 20% is a [claim].

## Step 5 · Launches: whisper, tease, shout (LD-091)
Treat affiliates like customers: "Give them something good, fast." A launch = affiliates advertise your lead magnet or core offer **before** it's available, then you sell to the assembled leads on launch day. "Do all the work for them."
| Phase | Think | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Whisper | call outs (curiosity, mystery, behind the scenes) | every **4–6 weeks** until **60 days** out; every **2–3 weeks** until **30 days** out |
| Tease | elements of value (reveal product, date, What-Who-When) | **weekly** until **14 days** out; **twice a week** until **3 days** out |
| Shout | call to action (bonuses, scarcity, urgency, guarantees) | **at least twice a day** from 3 days out; **every few hours** on the day until **2 hours** out; then **every 30 minutes** until launch |
[our note] The cadences are the author's; no response data. They assume the audience tolerates that many messages (see give : ask ratio, concept 14). The author presents this as the way to launch anything, not only affiliates.

## Step 6 · Integration: three ways, easiest to hardest (LD-092, figure)
1. Affiliates **give away your lead magnet** with every purchase of their stuff; then **you upsell** your core offer. (Free massage with personal training; free nutrition consult with every new membership.)
2. Affiliates **sell your lead magnet** separately (book, event, service, sample); they keep the cash from selling it; you profit by selling your main thing for more than the lead magnet costs; no split on the core offer (p.340).
3. Affiliates **sell your core offer**: **then you split the money** (upfront cash, all cash for a period, or all cash forever). The author prefers paying forever; "I never cap payouts."
- [FLAG] The payout caption "…then you split the money…" sits on p.340 between Way 2 and Way 3 headings and conflicts with Way 2's text; this file places it with Way 3. **A tool must not map "pay forever / split cash" to a specific way without re-checking p.340.**
- [our note] Paying "forever" with no cap is a liability that grows with each customer; **include payout duration and cap as inputs.**
- Pro Tip (image-only): **white-label lead magnets**: let affiliates use your lead magnets as theirs (e.g., meal plans, grocery lists without your logo).
- What the author uses: Strategy 1 twice a year as a big event, and Strategy 3 ongoing; many portfolio companies use Strategy 2. Make the offer good enough that refusing would feel foolish.

## Cases (LD-093, LD-094, all [claim])
- Service: tax/business-formation firm: launch = seminar offering a **free LLC setup**; integrate = affiliates include it in their core offer; cost = delivering the free LLC plus a percentage of every first sale.
- Physical: Prestige Labs supplements via gym owners: a gym signing 20 clients/month with 70% buying = **14** supplement customers/month/gym; "4000 gyms × 14 × $200" ([derived] $11.2M/month). [FLAG] assumes all licensed gyms participate (400+ active); an illustration of multiplication only.
- Local: chiropractor workshop promoted by a gym owner; 30 attendees × $99 = **$2,970**; integrate = one to two adjustments with every new membership. [note] "Splits" vs $2,970 being 100% of the gate is a tension.
- **Prestige Labs data (LD-094, figure p.318):** investment over $1,000,000 software + $3,000,000 inventory; first three weeks $150,000, then ~$380,000–$452,000/week. Weekly gross $429,112 · $383,717 · $411,848 · $404,836 · $452,204; refunds $21,948 · $25,644 · $20,651 · $20,719 · $22,222 (4.9%–6.7% of gross [derived]); orders 2,266 · 2,052 · 2,084 · 2,124 · 2,367; average order $189 · $187 · $198 · $191 · $191; active affiliates 428 · 409 · 418 · 437 · 444. [derived] "300 orders per day" is a five-week mean of 311, not a floor. [FLAG] text vs table not fully aligned; use the table.

## Costs and returns (LD-095)
Compare what it costs to get an affiliate with the gross profit of all customers they send. Worked example [stated]: **CAC per affiliate $4,000**; affiliate sells **$10,000/month for 12 months = $120,000**; 75% gross margin → COGS $30,000; gross profit $90,000; payout **40% of gross profit = $36,000**; leftover = $120,000 − $30,000 − $36,000 = **$54,000**.
- The book says "$54,000 ÷ $4,000 = **12.5 : 1**." [FLAG: arithmetic] It is **13.5 : 1**. Use 13.5.
- Targets: at least **3:1** for a decent business; want 5:1, 10:1+. If below 3:1: lower CAC; get more affiliates to **activate** (a launch process); improve integration.
- [our note] "LTGP" here is **net of the payout**; the example ignores program-running costs (Prestige Labs spent over $1,000,000).

## Conclusion (LD-096)
Affiliates "aren't an advertising method you can 'do'"; you use the core four to get them and treat them like customers. Your results depend on the goodwill you hold with your affiliate partners. Action: keep advertising your affiliate offer until you have **ten to twenty affiliates**, get results, and work out the problems. Order that "happens naturally": referrals → employees → agencies → affiliates. Expectation: to get a new lead source working within **three to six months** [stated]; compare cold outreach "almost a year."

## Do not
- Do not state a payout ratio without saying which LTGP definition.
- Do not offer "pay forever" without caps or duration inputs.
- Do not charge affiliates for certification without checking local rules.
- Do not use 7.8M, 12.5:1, 11.2M/month as results.
