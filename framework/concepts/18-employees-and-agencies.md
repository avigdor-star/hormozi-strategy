## EMPLOYEES

### Definition and leverage (LD-077)
- Lead-getting employees = people in your business whom you train to get you leads the way you did: run ads, make and post content, do outreach. [stated]
- Leverage math [stated]: trade **40 hours of doing for 4 hours of managing**; at scale **200 hours/week of work for 20 hours of management**; then a manager who costs you **4 hours/week** = 4 hours of your effort producing 200 hours of lead-getting ([derived] 50:1).
- Wealth argument [stated]: two businesses with **$5,000,000 revenue and $2,000,000 profit**: one runs only with you ("a high paying job"); one runs without you is "an asset… easily worth **$10,000,000+**" (the author's estimate; [derived] 5x profit). Earning makes you rich; owning makes you wealthy.
- [FLAG] The $10M value is the author's estimate; no valuation method in the book. Not a benchmark.

### The bottleneck case (LD-076, [claim])
Cold outreach missed its goal two quarters running. Trace backward: lose a rep every 4 weeks; HR says churn below industry average; hire **1 of every 4** candidates; **1 qualified per 10 screening interviews** ([derived] **40 interviews per hire** ✓). Fix: group interviews, push anyone with good work ethic and basic social skills to sales. Result: "Within six weeks, hiring outpaced churn"; cold outreach sales doubled and became more than half of sales.
Lesson [stated]: the shortfall had nothing to do with how cold outreach was done, the skill involved or the offer; there just were not enough people doing it. [derived] Method: trace the shortfall backward one funnel stage at a time until you find the limiting stage. One company, one quarter.

### Training: Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate (LD-079, figure)
1. **Document:** write a checklist of the steps exactly as you do the job; follow **only** the checklist next work block ("first draft").
2. **Demonstrate:** do it in front of them step by step; adjust the checklist when they slow you down ("second draft").
3. **Duplicate:** they do it in front of you with the same checklist; fix it until right.
- Standard: would a stranger who followed only your checklist get your results if you disappeared tomorrow?
- Rules: if they get it wrong, **we** got it wrong; judge **ability to follow directions** more than results ("it's the directions"); praise each correct step; don't take over when they mess up; feedback **one step at a time**; avoid penalties in training; retrain on any major dip.
- Image-only Pro Tip: "Give Short Windows For People To Prove Themselves": someone who has been trained properly and is still below expectations after three weeks should be cut.
- [our note] The three-week cut is the author's practice with no measured standard; **employment-law and contract rules are not discussed.**

### Returns and diagnosis (LD-080)
- **Cost per engaged lead = total payroll ÷ total engaged leads** (excluding ad spend). Example: $100,000 ÷ 1,000 = **$100**; if **1 in 10** engaged leads becomes a customer, **CAC = $1,000**; with **LTGP $4,000** → **4:1**. [derived ✓]
- [claim] Acquisition.com content team: about 30,000 engaged leads/month at about $100,000/month = about **$3.33 per engaged lead** ([derived ✓]).
- Efficiency rule: CAC **within 3x industry average** → doing good enough, focus on LTGP; **more than 3x** → sales problem or advertising problem (same 3x threshold as concept 16, but worded differently: LD-060 says to focus on advertising, LD-080 says "a sales problem or an advertising problem"; do not treat as one rule).
- **Diagnosis question:** do my engaged leads face the issue I solve and have money to pay for it? No → advertising problem. Yes and buying but not enough → advertising problem. Yes but not buying → **sales problem.** Do not fire the salesperson over what is really an advertising problem.
- Closing rule: costs "at least one-third of the profit." [FLAG] Reads backward; the meaning matching LTGP:CAC ≥ 3 is **CAC at most one-third of LTGP**. Use LTGP ÷ CAC ≥ 3.

### Conclusion (LD-081)
Whom you pick matters less than how you train those you take on. Be picky only for "hyper-specific multiple-six-figure C-suite employees." [our note] "Hire anyone willing" + "cut at three weeks" is the author's high-volume approach; fairness, law and turnover cost are not addressed (compare the churn case).

## AGENCIES

### What they are and when to hire (LD-082)
- Agencies = "lead-getting service businesses"; you pay them to run paid ads, do outreach, or package and distribute content. [stated]
- The agency cycle [stated, author's experience with 10+ agencies]: excited → onboarding → best senior rep → some results → senior rep moves to the newest customer → junior rep manages your account → results suffer → you complain → senior returns occasionally → you cancel → you search again. Conclusion: agencies can be valuable, though not in the way they want you to use them.
- **When:** good agencies cost money, so with no money agencies are not an option. Use for **learning new methods** and **learning new platforms** (a learning-transfer model, not outsourcing). [claim] Origin: $750/hour; the first four hours up front ($3,000); eight weekly calls = $6,000 ✓.

### The agreement (LD-083)
Opening script: ask to work with them for **6 months** to learn how they do it, offer to **pay extra** for an explanation of why they make their decisions, and say that when they are skilled enough you would like to shift to a **cheaper consulting arrangement**.
- Rules: give each agency relationship a purpose and a deadline from the start; be willing to negotiate.
- [claim] Two-agency pattern (second at **4x the price** to teach in-depth). Timeline: team took "about a year" to beat an agency, then ten months, eight, now "six months or less" (the book's wording is garbled).
- Why it works: better short-term (they know more) and long-term (your team learns); an agency gives only "a fraction of the attention." measure your own team against the agency until your team outperforms it, then cancel the relationship.
- Six next steps: decide if it makes sense · talk to many ("Don't be cheap") · use the agreement framework · set a clear deadline · use both teams until yours beats theirs · switch to discounted consulting, "then cut 'em loose."
- Cost note: expect a stretch in which you pay both the agency and your own team for the same work.
- [FLAG] Assumes the agency agrees to train you out of the business ("most agencies are not opposed"; no data). The comparison needs a shared metric: cost per engaged lead and LTGP:CAC (concepts 16 and above).

### Ten criteria for picking one (LD-084)
1 Somebody you know got good results (agencies known only from their own ads are probably worse than word-of-mouth ones) · 2 Prominent companies got results · 3 A **waiting list** · 4 Clear sales process, "no funny business" · 5 No short-term hacks; clear timelines · 6 Tell you exactly what they need from you · 7 Regular meeting schedule · 8 Simple updates and clear tracking · 9 A good **offer** judged by the four value drivers · 10 They are expensive: every good agency is expensive, but not every expensive agency is good. Even after agreement, talk to a few more.
[our note] Criterion 9 reuses the Value Equation (concept 04); criterion 3 is a scarcity signal (concept 06).
