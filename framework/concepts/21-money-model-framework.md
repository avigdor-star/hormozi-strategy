# Concept 21 · The Money Model (framework, stages, how to build one)

**Books:** Money Models (the home). Offers' "money model / payment terms" component of the Grand Slam Offer points here. Leads' 30-day cash rule is the same idea (concept 01).
**Built from entries:** MM-001, MM-002, MM-003, MM-004, MM-005, MM-006, MM-007, MM-063, MM-064, MM-065, MM-066, MM-067, MM-068, MM-008 and MM-069 (coverage notes: front matter, free-gift boxes, disclaimer; no usable theory).
**Tag key:** [stated] · [figure] · [derived] · [FLAG] · [claim] · [our note].

## Definitions (use the book's own)
- "A Money Model is a sequence of offers." Core: look for every chance to fix a customer's problem and then offer the fix. Make the offer when the customer realizes they need it, and there is no cap on how many offers you can make. [stated, MM-003]
- Section VI: A Money Model is a planned order of offers: what to offer, when, and how, to earn as much as possible as quickly as possible. Ideally, one customer brings in enough to win and serve at least two more customers within 30 days. [stated, MM-064]
- Recap line 1: A series of offers built to raise the number of customers, the amount each pays, and the speed of payment. (MM-068)
- Recap lines 2–3: A good Money Model makes more profit from a customer than it costs to acquire and serve them, with the window given as "in the first 30 days" (the book does not say whether that window limits the profit, the cost, or both). That is only the bare minimum. A **$100M** Money Model brings in, from one customer within 30 days, more profit than it costs to acquire and serve many customers, so cash stops being a limit. (MM-068)

## Why it exists (MM-004)
- Failure sequence [stated]: costs more to get a buyer than the profit you make → spend more than you made → cut advertising → fewer customers → float with personal cash/loans/credit → "pray for profit" → sell percentages → "lose it all."
- Option 1: wait two years for the money back. Option 2: get paid quickly and grow as big as you want. "A good Money Model is option 2."
- 30-day cash rule (author note): The author likes to recover the cost of winning a customer within 30 days. Reason: a credit card gives any business interest-free money for 30 days, if the balance is cleared before the month ends.
- Growth claim [stated as a question]: twice as valuable × twice as many × twice as fast = 8x; triple = 27x. [derived ✓] Holds only if the three factors multiply independently; no evidence in the book.
- **Definition of the 30-day test: see concept 01.** It has nine mentions, no formula. **The user must pick one definition before any calculator.**

## The four offer types (MM-005, figure)
| # | Type | Job [stated] |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Attraction | "turn strangers into customers" (concept 22) |
| 2 | Upsell | "get people to spend more cash" (concept 23) |
| 3 | Downsell | turn a would-be no into a yes (concept 24) |
| 4 | Continuity | "keep people buying" (concept 25) |
- Use a single offer type, two, several of the same type, or all four at once. Rationale in order: no customers without attraction; upsell gets "some cash"; downsells turn "nos" into "yeses"; continuity makes extra cash "guaranteed to come in month after month."
- [claim, no data] One offer beats none, and more offers beat one. The next sentence in MM-005, which says they work well alone but together make the business unstoppable, is also a [claim].
- [FLAG] The order is a reading order, not a required timeline; offers can be used alone, whenever you like, and in any sequence (Note 7). Upsells and downsells are used inside attraction chapters.

## The seven Important Notes (MM-006)
1. All businesses have money models. 2. Some work better in some businesses; you have to design your own. 3. **If a customer asks for their money back, give it back.** 4. Hard selling is for weak products; make the offer when the customer has the problem. 5. **Obey the law** (rules on advertising keep tightening, most of all around the word free; check with lawyers). 6. **Be transparent** ("State the facts… Don't lie."). 7. Any offer can be used on its own, at any time, in any order.
[FLAG] Note 7 says "any order" and "the right sequence"; treat sequence as a design choice. Note 6 sits next to tactics that frame outcomes so customers feel like winners (see concept 22); flag each when reached.

## Three stages (MM-064)
- **Stage I: Get Cash** (Attraction: "get more customers for less"). **Stage II: Get More Cash** (Upsell and Downsell). **Stage III: Get The Most Cash** (Continuity).
- Why staged: a "finished" money model on day one "will collapse on top of you." None of the author's businesses began with a complete Money Model; each began at Stage I.
- Order of build: get customers reliably → they pay for themselves reliably → they pay for other customers reliably → maximize long-term value → spend as many ad dollars as you can. The author makes each stage pay for the next; when the Money Model begins to work, the business begins to break.
- [our note] The stage order is the author's usual order; continuity can come earlier (MM-053, MM-054, MM-067 "There are no rules").

## The four steps to make yours (MM-066)
1. **Attraction:** "turn strangers into customers and cover our costs." Choose from the five (concept 22) and advertise it. Finding the best choice can take as long as a year.
2. **Upsell:** make 30-day profits far above the cost of winning a new customer and delivering to them. Choose from four. Present the offer when the customer's need is at its highest.
3. **Downsell:** "get customers who said no… to say yes." Same price → change how they pay (payment plans, trials); lower price → change what they get (feature downsell). "Alternate between them in the same sale."
4. **Continuity:** add a final sale inside the 30-day window and layer on recurring cash. Best timing may be after day 30.
- [FLAG] Step 4: "in our 30-day window" and "after the first thirty days"; the book accepts both. Step 1's "cover our costs" does not define which costs.
- Boxed note: A business funding itself must win customers at a profit, and a Money Model is the sole route to profitable scaling.

## Rules for building (MM-067)
- **Perfect one offer at a time:** Stay on your current stage, choose a single offer, repeat it until it works reliably, and judge progress over quarters rather than weeks. Trying to put in a full Money Model all at once will break the business.
- **Raise price in stages:** start cheap for yeses and feedback; then increase the price step by step until you earn less. [FLAG] Conflicts with the 30-day break-even test (lower price = lower 30-day cash); the book's stop rule is empirical.
- **"Simple Scales. Fancy Fails.":** "100 ways to offer your product," not 100 products (e.g., one, two, three, four sessions per week).
- **Affiliate products can fill gaps** (sell others' stuff for a commission). [FLAG: disclosure] Commission disclosure is not mentioned. See Leads' affiliates (concept 19).
- **Turn attraction into continuity with automatic renewal** (e.g., Buy 6 Get 6 Free → month-to-month). [FLAG] Auto-renewal: disclosure/consent not discussed (Dog Food: "Cancel anytime!"). Payments-risk area.
- **Mix and match:** "There are no rules." Start the suggested way; experiment later.

## Worked examples (MM-063, MM-065) [claim unless noted]
- **Gym Launch story:** Stage 1 Decoy (free courses/calls → $16,000 over 16 weeks) which brought the author to $476,000 per month after three months; Classic Upsell (Gym Lords $42,000/yr; $6,000 prepay discount) + Continuity Bonus + Payment Plan Downsell + Continuity Discount → "~$1,500,000 per month"; Menu Upsell + Feature Downsells → "$2,300,000 per month. All within 14 months"; Prestige Labs by month 20 "$4,400,000 per month." [FLAG] Not stated whether revenue, cash or profit; the last combines two businesses. **Not benchmarks.** [derived] $42,000 − $6,000 = $36,000 prepaid ✓.
- **Breakdown [stated]:** Gym Launch: Decoy ($16,000 DWY) → Classic Upsell ($42,000/yr; $36,000 prepaid) → Payment Plan Downsell ($10,000 down, $800/week for 52 weeks) → Menu Close + Feature Downsell (Full Package $800/week: advertising $300 + sales training $200 + new releases $500 + licensing materials $100 = $1,100 [derived]; Minimum Package $100/week). Micro Gyms: Win Your Money Back; Split Pay → Three-Pay → Free Trial With Penalty; Menu Upsell (supplements); Feature Downsell; Rollover + Lifetime Discount ($50 off per month for life with a 12-month commitment). Newsletter: Free Trial ($0 then $399/month after 30 days) + Pay Less Now/More Later + Lifetime Discount (pay $297 today and lock in that rate for life), "a six-headed money-making monster." Dog Food: Buy 4 Months Get 2 Free; Classic Upsell (toys, vitamins); Feature Downsell; auto-renewal month-to-month.
- [FLAG] Minimum package is "a discounted monthly rate" (story) vs "$100 per week" (breakdown). $800/week appears twice (payment plan and Full Package): different offers. $297 "now" is not stated as one-time vs monthly (likely monthly vs $399). Stage labels in the breakdowns don't match the stage definitions (e.g., Menu Close under Stage III).
- [FLAG] Full Package $800 vs listed features $1,100: $300/week unexplained.

## Origin stories (MM-001, MM-002, MM-003)
- **Storage-unit:** "free" first month; the front desk says it costs the customer **$127** ($47 lock + boxes/tape + insurance etc.; the $127 cannot be rebuilt from the book). Principle: once you learn how to earn in one business, you spot ways to earn in any business. [claim] The mechanism is attraction + need-time add-ons.
- **Gym funnel:** "Free 6 Week Challenge" at **$5/lead**; half show, half of those buy a **$600** program → 25% of leads become customers ([derived] $20 per customer); plus **$80** profit from supplements → **$680**; 680 ÷ 20 = **34** ("one dollar in… 34 out"). [FLAG] $680 mixes revenue ($600) and profit ($80): the 34:1 is neither a clean return on ad spend nor a clean profit multiple; timing ("48 hours" vs "thirty days") is not reconciled; "$20,000 of monthly memberships for $3,000 down" can't be recomputed.
- **Rental car (December 2019):** Vehicle Upgrade → Late Return → Premium Insurance → Minimum Insurance Downsell → Prepaid Gas. The author wanted a $19/day car but left paying $100/day, a 5x gap. [derived] 100/19 = 5.26. [FLAG] per-day vs total price in different places; prepaid fuel $3.75 vs pump $3.50 (about 7% above).

## Cross-book
- Offers: the money model is a named component of the Grand Slam Offer (OF-005). Offers' commodity-vs-Grand Slam funnel (OF-011) uses cash payback.
- Leads: client-financed acquisition (LD-061) is the same 30-day cash rule; Leads gives only the headline, MM gives the offers.
- The book map [stated]: Offers = what to sell; Leads = how to find the people; Money Models = how to get them to buy ("A Money Model").

## Do not
- Do not tell a client "cover your costs in 30 days" without choosing the definition (concept 01).
- Do not build all four stages at once.
- Do not use 34:1, 5x, $476,000 or $4,400,000 as benchmarks.
