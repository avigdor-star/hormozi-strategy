# Concept 02 · Market selection (and niche)

**Books:** Offers (Ch.4). Leads touches it only in passing (see Cross-book).
**Built from entries:** OF-014, OF-015, OF-016, OF-017, OF-018, OF-019 (also OF-012).

## What it is
A way to judge where to sell before judging what to sell. The book ranks three levers: **Market > Offer > Persuasion Skills** (OF-016). Each is rated **great / normal / bad**.

## Inputs a tool can ask for (all user judgment, none computed)
| Input | Allowed values | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Market type | great / normal / bad | OF-014 |
| Offer strength | great / normal / bad | OF-016 |
| Persuasion skill | great / normal / bad | OF-016 |
| Four market indicators | massive pain · purchasing power · easy to target · growing | OF-015 |

## Rules (author-stated)
1. A "great" at a higher-order piece **overpowers** anything lower. A "normal" **passes it to the next** piece. A "bad" **stops the equation**, unless a "great" from a higher-priority component nullifies it. [stated, OF-016]
2. Three author examples [stated, OF-016]:
   - bad offer + bad persuasion in a great market → makes money;
   - normal market + great offer → "tons of money" even with bad persuasion;
   - normal market + normal offer → needs exceptional persuasion ("hardest path").
3. You cannot be in a "bad" market or "nothing will work." [stated, OF-016]
4. The framework is built on the assumption that you are in a market that is at least normal. [stated, OF-014]
5. Demand is **channeled, not created.** [stated, OF-014]

### Decision table
| Market | Offer | Persuasion needed |
|---|---|---|
| bad | any | nothing can rescue it |
| great | any | not needed [stated] |
| normal | great | not needed [stated] |
| normal | normal | must be exceptional [stated] |
| normal | bad | stops [derived: the book does not state this row] |

[FLAG] The "bad stops unless nullified" sentence is ambiguous for a bad offer in a normal market. We read it as "stops" (OF-016).

## Market definitions
- **Normal** = growing at the same rate as the marketplace, with common unmet needs in **health, wealth or relationships**. [stated]
- **Great** = a "starving crowd" (large, intense demand). **Bad** = shrinking or dying (newspapers, "shrinking by 25 percent every year"). Both by example only. [stated]
- [FLAG] **No numeric cut-offs** separate great / normal / bad. "Marketplace growth rate" is never defined (Ch.3 gives the stock market's ~9%/yr, OF-012, but the book never links it to "normal market"). Treat as a user judgment.

## The four indicators (OF-015)
1. **Massive pain**: they "desperately need," not "want." Pain is proportional to the price you can charge.
2. **Purchasing power**: they can afford it or can reach the money.
3. **Easy to target**: they gather somewhere you can reach.
4. **Growing**: tailwind vs. headwind.

Failure examples in the book: no purchasing power (resume help for the unemployed); shrinking (newspapers).

**Not in the book (do not build in):**
- a numeric score for any indicator;
- weights between indicators;
- a rule that "any one missing = disqualified."

The closing line is: if all three criteria score well and the market is not getting smaller, your position will be solid. [stated]

[our note] The offer-growth-coach skill's "flag when any indicator is missing" is not stated in the book. Check it in the later audit.

## Niche rules (OF-017, OF-018, OF-019)
- Pick one niche and stay long enough for trial and error. **Duration is not specified.** Do not hard-code a time. [stated]
- **Under $10M/yr revenue: niching down makes more money** (author's statement). Above that it "depends on how narrow the niche is" (TAM). To grow past TAM: up market, down market, or an adjacent market. [stated]
- Narrower buyer → higher price for near-identical content. Price ladder [figure]: $19 → $99 → $499 → $1,997. The text's "25x / 100x" are rounded; real ratios are 26.3x and 105x. Use the figure's prices. These are illustrations, not benchmarks. (OF-018)
- Avatar specificity template [stated, OF-019]: business size, owned asset, staffing, goal. Example: microgym owner, about 100 members, signed lease, at least one employee, wants to help clients lose weight.
- [claim] "$10M/yr is a top .4% achievement" (unsourced in Ch.4). "Many companies passed $30M/yr in a single niche."
