# Concept 12 · The Core Four, More-Better-New, and the roadmap

**Books:** Leads (the book's central model). Offers' market>offer>persuasion order sits above it. Money Models supplies "make them worth more."
**Built from entries:** LD-001, LD-003, LD-016, LD-064, LD-067, LD-068, LD-100, LD-101, LD-102, LD-103, LD-104 (see also concepts 13–16, 17–19, 20). (other IDs cited in the body include: LD-024, LD-059, LD-063, LD-065, LD-096, LD-099)
**Tag key:** [stated] · [figure] · [derived] · [FLAG] · [claim] · [our note].

## Growth frame (LD-001, LD-003)
Two ways to grow a business: get more customers; make them worth more. Leads covers the first; it ends at the **engaged lead** ("raised hand") and does **not** cover closing. You get more customers by more, better, cheaper, **reliable** leads. [stated] Offers' version: more customers, higher average purchase, more purchases (concept 01).

## The Core Four (LD-016, figure)
| | Warm (gave you permission) | Cold (didn't) |
|---|---|---|
| 1-to-1 private | Warm outreach (concept 13) | Cold outreach (concept 15) |
| 1-to-many public | Post free content (concept 14) | Run paid ads (concept 16) |
- The core four are the only four ways to make anyone aware of anything. Falling short on leads means the core four are being done with too little skill or too little volume. [stated] No numeric test for either.
- Automation doesn't change the nature of the channel.
- Which to start with (LD-068): after warm outreach gets the first customers, the author turns to posting content when he has more time than money, and to cold outreach or ads when he has more money than time. Only one is needed: pick one, then max it out by doing more, doing better, and doing new. [stated]
- Compounding (every combination boosts the others) is a [claim]. Advertising is a trade of **time, money, or both**.
- [FLAG] "Do paid ads last" (LD-063) vs the author's history (paid ads early for gyms; LD-068): treat "last" as advice for learners with little money.

## More, Better, New (LD-064, LD-067)
- [FLAG] The text says "two ways" then lists **three**: More (do more of what you're doing), Better, New. Use three. Order: More first.
- Typical stall: companies reach "$1M–$3M per year" with one platform and assume they've "tapped" their market. **Size of the Pie fallacy** [figure]: owners think the market is split among a few competitors; really there are four full circles (one per core-four method), each with many platforms inside. [claim] Framing cases (a $2M chiropractor spending $30,000/month on one platform; industry sizes unsourced).
- It is far easier to claim you have reached your size limit than to admit you are worse at advertising than you believed. [stated]
- **When to do new:** Do new when the returns from doing more or better fall below what a new placement or a new way of advertising could return. Order, nine times out of ten: **new placements → new platforms → new core-four activity.** [our note] No numeric stop rule; the user must estimate returns for each option.
- Diagnostic question before investing: what is holding them back from doing ten times their current amount of activity?
- Details of More (Rule of 100) and Better (constraints, weekly test): concept 20.

## One-page plan (LD-100, figure)
1. Pick the type of engaged lead: customers, affiliates, employees, or agencies. 2. Pick **Rule of 100** or **Open to Goal** and commit daily. 3. Fill out the advertising checklist for that daily action. 4. Do it daily until you can afford to pay someone else to do it. 5. Then go back to step 1 with **employees** as the target; repeat until you have the help you need; scale again.
- Figure "Rule of 100": 100/day × 100 days; outbound warm = reach outs; outbound cold = reach outs; inbound warm = minutes of content; inbound cold = **dollars** of paid ads.
- [FLAG] Paid ads' "100" is **minutes** in LD-065 but **dollars** here; "When = first 8 hours" here vs "first four hours" in warm outreach (LD-024) and 8 hours in LD-099. **Ask the user which unit and time block.**
- The one-page advertising checklist takes about five minutes to fill out. [stated]

## The seven levels (LD-101)
| Level | Description | Primary action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Friends know about the stuff you sell; one offer, avatar, platform | warm outreach |
| 2 | Consistent customers from maximizing **personal** work capacity | max warm outreach and content |
| 3 | **Employees** help you advertise; you've maxed personal inputs | hire people to advertise |
| 4 | Product good enough for consistent **referrals**: "shoot for **25% or more**" from referrals | fix the product until referrals are consistent |
| 5 | More places, ways, people; at least two methods on multiple platforms | scale advertising |
| 6 | "You hire killers": executives/department heads | take over new channels |
| 7 | Not written | — |
- The $100M+ machine includes a setup in which a third of customers bring in more customers. [FLAG] 25% (Level 4) vs a third (machine): both targets, unsourced. Time: "five to ten years" for owners who know what to do (author: more than ten years). [claim]
- [our note] Do not assign revenue bands to levels. The levels are an ordering of effort types, not an assessment test. They combine the lead-getter order from LD-096 (referrals → employees → agencies → affiliates).

## The author's own summary (LD-102)
Seven bullets: engaged leads · lead magnet/offer · core four · More-Better-New (with "ten times the volume" and "find the constraint") · four lead getters · Rule of 100 / Open to Goal / one-page plan · seven levels. Result claimed: "more, better, cheaper, reliable engaged leads."

## The closing principle (LD-103)
Parable of two dice (20 vs 200 sides): "If you quit, you lose." "**You cannot lose if you do not quit.**" [FLAG] The parable assumes a viable offer in a viable market and costless rolls. In Offers a bad market stops the equation (OF-016); Leads itself says to shut off losing ads (LD-059). It is motivational, not a decision rule.

## What this copy does not contain (LD-104)
Three promised cold scripts; downloadable one-page checklist; free training; "Scaling from $0 to $100M+" bonus tutorial; "Your First Avatar"; the sales/persuasion book. **Mark as missing; never invent them.**
