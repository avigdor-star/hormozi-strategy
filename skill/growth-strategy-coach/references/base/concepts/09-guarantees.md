# Concept 09 · Guarantees (risk reversal) and the refund-linked offers

**Books:** Offers (Ch.15, the home). Money Models (guarantees used inside attraction, upsell and downsell offers). Leads (guarantee slots in scripts; guarantee tied to actions).
**Built from entries:** OF-065, OF-066, OF-067, OF-068, OF-069, OF-070, OF-071, OF-072, OF-073 · MM-009–013, MM-011, MM-013, MM-018, MM-023, MM-025, MM-031, MM-039, MM-049, MM-051, MM-006 · LD-020, LD-034, LD-072. (other IDs cited in the body include: MM-017, LD-003)
**Tag key:** [stated] · [figure] · [derived] · [FLAG] · [claim] · [our note].

## Why (OF-065)
- "The single greatest objection… is risk." Reversing risk is the top way to lift an offer's conversion; experienced marketers spend as much time on guarantees as on deliverables. [stated, assertion]
- Claim: one marketer saw conversion rise **2–4x** just by changing the guarantee. [claim, hearsay]
- Always push your guarantee strongly, even if you lack one; state it with confidence and explain the reasoning behind it. [stated]
- Guarantees are **enhancers, not a business**: used to cover a poor product or sales team they "backfire into lots of refunds." [stated]

## Structure
**If the customer misses result X within time period Y, the seller does Z.** The "or what" (Z) gives teeth; without it, "weak and diluted." [stated, OF-067]
Weak → stronger: "We will get you 20 clients guaranteed" → 20 clients within their first 30 days, or the money back plus the advertising dollars they spent with the seller.

## Four types (OF-067)
| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| Unconditional | Strongest; in effect a paid-first trial in which the customer decides later whether they like it. More buy, some refund. |
| Conditional | Has terms; should be "better than money back"; match their investment with an equal or higher perceived commitment; make key success actions part of the conditions. |
| Anti-guarantee | Explicit "all sales are final," owned with a creative reason why. |
| Implied | Performance-based: "if I don't perform, I don't get paid." |
Choose by business [stated]: broader guarantees for **lower-ticket B2C**; the higher the ticket and the more business-oriented, the more **specific** (refund optional, conditions optional). High-cost services: you take a loss on both the refunded money and the cost of delivering, so lean conditional or anti-.
Claim: given a refund or the outcome, the great majority of people will pick the outcome. [claim]

## Guarantee math (OF-066)
- Net sales = sales × (1 − refund rate). [derived]
- Example [stated]: no strong guarantee = 100 sales, 5 refunds, net 95; strong guarantee = 130 sales, 13 refunds (10%), net 117. 117 ÷ 95 = **1.23x**. [derived ✓]
- **Break-even** [derived]: `sales multiple × (1 − new refund rate) = (1 − old refund rate)`. For 5% → 10%, sales must rise ×1.056 (+5.6%). The book's own break-even example (105 sales, 10 refunds, net 95, refund rate 9.5%) agrees.
- [FLAG] The phrase "an increase of 5 percent" mixes percent of sales with counts; use the formula.
- [our note] The math ignores fulfillment cost per refunded customer; add COGS when delivery has real cost.
- Cautions [stated]: guarantee-driven buyers "can become very shitty customers" and may not do the work; tie the guarantee to the actions they must take.

## Stacking (OF-068)
- Unconditional + conditional: 30-day no-questions refund **plus** a conditional **triple-your-money-back 90-day** guarantee.
- Two conditional guarantees on different or sequential outcomes (for example, $10,000 within 60 days and $30,000 within 90 days, provided the customer completes steps 1, 2 and 3): shifts risk back to the seller.

## Catalog (OF-069, OF-070)
**Unconditional**
1. "No Questions Asked": full refund; 50%; refund of ad spend and ancillary costs; you pay for a competitor's program; money back plus an additional amount. Risky; the more conditions, the faster it "loses its teeth."
2. Satisfaction-based refund: the author's own for weight-loss programs, framed as guaranteeing $500 of value. [claim] **2 refund requests in 4,000 sales over 3.5 years** (0.05%). Best for **lower-ticket**; very risky for high-ticket and high-fulfillment-cost.
3. Name it with vivid imagery, not vanilla "satisfaction."
**Conditional** (author says he used all except the hotel/phone-call one)
| Name | Client gets | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Outsized refund | Double/triple money back or $X | High-margin; add a consumption condition |
| Service guarantee | You keep working free **until X is achieved** | "Personal favorite"; removes the time element; add conditions (set up a page, attend calls, weigh in, report data) |
| Modified service | Another Y of service/access free; Y generally **at least twice** the duration | Finite version |
| Credit-based | Money returned as credit toward any service | Best during **upsell** |
| Personal service | You work one-on-one free until X | Add conditions |
| Hotel + airfare perks | Reimburse product plus hotel and airfare | Workshops/events. **Not author-tested** |
| Wage-payment | Pay their hourly rate if the session wasn't valuable | Claim: you never actually end up paying one of these out. [FLAG: ethics] |
| Release of service | Let them out of the contract free | Where contracts are enforceable |
| Delayed second payment | Don't bill again until first outcome | Needs a known **activation metric** |
| First outcome | You keep paying ancillary costs until first outcome | Focuses everyone on the first result |
- [FLAG] Wage-payment: "1,960 hours = 40 hrs/wk for a year." 40 × 52 = **2,080**; 1,960 = 40 × 49. Use 2,080 or state the assumption.
- [claim] Fladlien example: $3M added sales minus 10 refunds × $25,000 = $2.75M ✓ (as stated). Course $2,997.

## Anti-guarantee: "All sales are final" (OF-071)
- For consumables or products that lose value once seen/used; frame as a **damaging admission**. Also for high-ticket products requiring work: tell buyers that anyone who needs a guarantee before taking the leap is not who you are looking for.
- [FLAG: ethics] The text says "Just make one up that sounds compelling." **A tool must require the stated reason to be true.**

## Implied guarantees (OF-072)
Performance ($ per sale/show/pound), revshare (10% of top-line; 20% profit share; 25% of growth over baseline), profit-share, ratchets (10% over X, 20% over Y, 30% over Z), triggers. Hybrids: whichever is higher, $1,000 or 10% of the revenue generated; **$1,000/month floor for 3 months, then 100% performance**. Needs **quantifiable outcomes**, **transparency**, and **trust or control** to be paid. Drawbacks: tracking and collection. [claim] Agencies "from $20k/mo to $200k+/mo."

## Designing yours (OF-073)
1. List the buyer's biggest fears, pain and perceived obstacles ("What are they most afraid of?"). 2. **Reverse each fear into a guarantee**; more specific and creative is better. 3. Start with **service-based guarantees or performance partnerships**; then keep and scale, or loosen to increase volume. 4. Then name the offer (concept 10).

## Money Models: guarantees inside offers
- **Important Note 3 (MM-006):** when a customer asks for a refund, give it. A policy, not a rate target. [FLAG] vs MM-039 "Do Rollover Upsells before refunding": the book doesn't say whether a refund is still owed if the customer declines the rollover. Our reading: offer rollover first; if they still ask, refund (not the book's words).
- **Win Your Money Back** (MM-009–013): set a goal; if met, refund as cash or store credit.
  - Rules [stated]: use it only when your refund rate is under 5%, otherwise fix the product; and offer it only if you are comfortable handing money back. About **10%** of customers "will ask for their money back" (unsourced [claim]).
  - [FLAG] 10% vs 5% not reconciled (MM-011). Store credit and cash got the same number of customers in the author's testing [claim]; to keep calling it free in advertising, combine it with a no-conditions satisfaction guarantee (an option, not a requirement).
  - [FLAG: economics] "Make everyone a winner" turns it into a deposit credited to the next purchase; profit then comes from the follow-on offer (MM-013).
- **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later** (MM-023, MM-025): delayed payment + **conditional** guarantee; cancel only if you qualify (attendance etc.). Trigger [stated]: if more than **10%** of Pay Later people cancel, either you overpromised, the guarantee's qualifying conditions are too lenient, or the price is too steep. [FLAG] "remove all risk" vs conditional; base of the 10% is people who chose pay later; "conditions are too low" is ambiguous. Taking and charging cards is a payments-risk area.
- **Charging for guarantees** (MM-031): add **5–50%** to the price for a guarantee/warranty/insurance. [claim] Art studio replaced portraits free; asked for 10% extra; 30% of customers bought.
- **Decoy Offer** (MM-017/018): the decoy version has "No support. No guarantee"; premium versions include guarantees. [FLAG] the premium's guarantee cost must be modeled by the user.
- **Feature Downsell** (MM-049/051): remove the guarantee in the cheaper version; because people value security, taking it away makes many of them see what it was worth. [FLAG] downsell buyers carry no refund exposure; the book doesn't model refund cost of full-price buyers.

## Leads
- LD-020: "guarantees are huge" for perceived likelihood; script line "I even guarantee…"; LD-034 core-offer script has both a refund **and** continued work with the customer until they do. [FLAG] Don't fill guarantee slots with promises the business won't honor.
- LD-072 lever 3 step 6: **match your guarantee's conditions** to the actions that produce the best results (cross-links OF-066 cautions).
- LD-003: advertising "lets you have a terrible product" (claim) conflicts with OF-065's warning that guarantees on a poor product backfire. Do not use it as a rule.

## Do not
- Do not offer a guarantee the business cannot afford; model refunds plus fulfillment cost.
- Do not generate a fake "reason why" for an anti-guarantee.
- Do not use 2–4x conversion, 0.05% refund rate, or $20k→$200k as expected results.
