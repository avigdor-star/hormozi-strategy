## What it is and why
- The audience, not the content you make, is the asset that compounds. Posting says it **once and reaches all**, so it yields more engaged leads per hour than warm outreach. People who find it valuable join your warm audience; shares bring more. [stated, LD-025]
- Three effects [stated]: grows your warm audience; a constantly growing audience "of people more likely to buy"; makes **all other advertising more effective**.
- Three trade-offs [stated]: harder to personalize (fewer respond); you compete with everyone posting; if you stand out, people copy you (keep innovating).
- [claim] Celebrity examples (Kylie Jenner, etc.) are survivor cases; no base rate. [our note] Use for the logic of audience-as-asset only.
- Second limit of warm outreach (list size) is what content solves (concept 13).

## Volume (LD-026; the advice is [stated], the two results are [claim])
Advice: keep posting steadily across all platforms, and simply do more. Result 1: 12 months → audience grew by "more than 200,000." Result 2: next 6 months at **ten times the content** → "added 1.2M." Rule: "Volume works."
[derived] 200,000 ÷ 12 ≈ 16,700/month; 1,200,000 ÷ 6 = 200,000/month → about **12x** the monthly rate, not exactly 10x.
[FLAG] One person's before/after on his own accounts; uncontrolled. Treat as a direction, not a multiplier.

## The content unit: hook, retain, reward (LD-027)
- Content grows an audience only by **rewarding** people: they need a reason to consume it, pay attention long enough, and get the reason satisfied. Reversed: **Hook** (get noticed) · **Retain** (get consumed) · **Reward** (satisfy the reason).
- **Content unit** = the least material that can hook, retain and reward an audience's attention. Can be as small as an image or a sentence.
- Hook effectiveness = "the percentage of people who start consuming." Raise it with interesting topics, headlines that give a reason, and matching the format of content they already like.

**Hook 1: five topic categories (LD-028):** far past ("the story without the scar") · recent past (do stuff, then talk about it; testimonials/case studies) · present (post ideas as they happen; make more on topics that beat normal) · trending (go where attention is, apply your experience) · manufactured (turn an idea into reality, e.g., living on $100 for a month; costs most, "biggest payouts"). Author prefers personal experience: "there's only one of you."

**Hook 2: headline components (LD-029)** [stated; unsourced "meta-analysis of news"]: recency · relevancy · celebrity · proximity · conflict · unusual · ongoing. [FLAG] "Try and include at least two" vs "Include one or more": use "at least one, two preferred" and say so. Format rule: shape the content for the platform first, then fine-tune it to catch the audience you want.

**Retain (LD-030):** curiosity via embedded unresolved questions. Three formats: **lists** (any order, give the number), **steps** (specific order, more explicit reward), **stories**. Claim: telling the number retains attention longer.

**Reward (LD-031):** "value per second"; length is never the problem, only boredom. You can't guarantee a reward but can raise its chance (right audience, retaining formats, satisfying the reason). Test: **"your audience grows."** [our note] A lagging whole-audience measure; no per-post metric.

**Short vs long (LD-032, figure):** same units, linked ("?" question, "!" payoff, heart reward). Start short, build up.

## Give : ask ratio (LD-033)
- "Jab, jab, jab, right hook." Deposit goodwill with rewarding content; withdraw by making offers. The author tries to "under-ask."
- Reference ratios [stated]: television 47 min giving per 13 min of ads ("roughly 3.5:1"; [derived] 3.6:1) · Facebook newsfeed ~4 content posts per ad (~4:1) · podcast example "well above 3:1."
- [FLAG] Three different floors (3.5, 4, 3). Use the explicit examples; no evidence any is the threshold where an audience shrinks.
- **Give until they ask:** give in public, ask in private; people "start asking you." [claim: opinion] As soon as you begin asking for money, you have chosen to slow your growth. [stated, assertion]

## How to ask (LD-034)
| Strategy | Definition | Example | Best on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Integrated | an ask inside each piece | hour podcast with 3 × 30-second ads = 58.5 min give : 1.5 min ask ([derived] **39:1**) | long-form |
| Intermittent | many pure "give" pieces, then an occasional "ask" piece | 10 give posts, then promote on the 11th (10:1 by count) | short-form |
- Integrated placement: "after a valuable moment or the end"; try one placement, check growth doesn't slow, then add more. [claim] A friend's podcast shrank after asking too often.
- What to ask: **core offer or lead magnet**. When unsure, pick the lead magnet because it carries less risk.
- Core-offer script (same logic as LD-020): say you are looking for 5 (avatar) within (time delay); they won't have to do (effort); if they don't get (dream outcome): 1) you refund their money 2) you work with them until they do. After the ask: "get back to providing value."
- [FLAG] The script includes a guarantee and a "looking for 5" scarcity line. Do not fill these with promises/caps the business won't honor.

## Scaling a warm audience (LD-035)
| | Depth then width | Width then depth |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | maximize one platform, then add another | be on all relevant platforms, then maximize all |
| Numbers (hedged upper ranges, not targets) | short-form "up to ten times per day per platform"; long-form "up to five days per week" | none |
| Pro | fewer resources, compounding, higher return per platform | broader reach faster, repurpose across platforms |
| Con | no omnipresence, **single-channel risk** | more labor, "sucky fluff" |
"Both are right." No rule for choosing; the trade-off is resources/risk vs speed/reach.

## Why do it even when paid ads are main (LD-036)
[claim] Paid ads stopped working "like it used to" (January 2020); the team concluded the author stopped making gym content. Survey [stated]: 78% of all clients had gone through at least TWO long-form content pieces before booking a call.
[FLAG] It covers clients who booked a call only (no comparison group): content was common among buyers, not shown to cause purchases. Treat as a hypothesis.

## Seven lessons (LD-037, mostly [claim])
1. "How I" instead of "How to." 2. "We need to be reminded more than taught" (1 in 5 hadn't known about his book). 3. Puddles → ponds → lakes → oceans (narrow first, expand stepwise; parallels Offers niche ladder, concept 02). 4. Content creates sales tools (a "greatest hits" list labeled with the problem solved). 5. Free content retains paying customers. 6. "Higher standards," not shorter attention spans. 7. Avoid pre-scheduling posts (author's theory).

## Benchmarks (LD-038)
Measure monthly: total followers and reach (how big); rate of growth = new ÷ started (how fast). Example: 1,000 → 1,500 followers = +500 = 50%; reach 10,000 → 15,000 = 50%. [derived ✓]
- "We can only control inputs": pick **posting cadence** and **ask cadence** per platform, then "Do. Not. Stop."
- [claim] Podcast twice a week for four years before reaching the Top 100.
- [our note] Cross-platform follower sums double-count people; "reach" is undefined (impressions vs unique). **No pass/fail benchmark for what monthly growth is "good."**

## First post and daily rhythm (LD-039)
First post can make an ask; if no engaged lead, give value for some time, then ask once you have earned the right. Do warm outreach and posting together daily; those two methods alone can, in time, grow a business to six or seven figures [stated]. Daily checklist [figure]: how much = **100 min per day**; when = every morning, 7 days a week; "give give give until they ask."
[our note] "100 min per day" is time; "100 attempts per day" in warm outreach is a count. Do not merge.

## Do not
- Do not use 3.5:1 / 4:1 / 12x / 78% / 50% growth as targets.
- Do not claim content caused sales from the 78% survey.
- Do not sum followers across platforms without saying so.
