# Concept 20 · Volume, constraints, testing, and "open to goal"

**Books:** Leads (Core Four on Steroids, Section V). Offers: testing names and offers. Money Models: [our note] tuning rules appear inside some offers (e.g. MM-048); the Leads entries make no such link.
**Built from entries:** LD-065, LD-066, LD-097, LD-098, LD-099, plus the per-method checklists in LD-024, LD-039, LD-048, LD-063; LD-009 (testing), LD-059 (test budget), OF-077.
**Tag key:** [stated] · [figure] · [derived] · [FLAG] · [claim] · [our note].

## The principle
"Advertising is an inputs and outputs game": advertising effort is the input and engaged leads are the output; even the correct action fails if done in the wrong amount. "Most people dramatically underestimate the volume it takes." [stated, LD-098]
Two moves: do **more** until it breaks, then make it **better** with small tests, then **new** (concept 12).

## More: the Rule of 100 (LD-065)
Doubling your inputs gets you more engaged leads, even with no improvements at all. **100 primary actions every day for 100 days in a row.** (the author says he makes few promises, yet this is one [claim: promise])
| Method | The 100 |
|---|---|
| Warm reach outs | 100 reach outs per day |
| Post content | 100 **minutes** per day making content |
| Cold reach outs | 100 reach outs per day |
| Paid ads | 100 **minutes** per day making ads; 100 days running (the checklist figure says "learning budget, then reverse to sales goal" and "30+ call outs × 10 ads") |
[FLAG] Two units (count vs minutes) and the paid-ads unit differs across pages (minutes in LD-065, dollars in LD-100). **Do not convert minutes to counts.** It is a rule of effort, not a statistical threshold.

## Better: constraints and the weekly test (LD-066)
- Do more "until it breaks" (CAC too high to sustain); then small tests. Winners differ from beginners by running thousands of such small tests.
- **Constraint** = a step where leads drop off: each step a lead takes on the way to becoming a customer is a potential point where they drop off; test at "whatever step the most leads drop off."
- Worked example [stated]: opt-in **30%**, apply **5%** (the constraint), schedule **50%**. Improve each by 5 points:
| Step | Before → after | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Opt-in | 30% → 35% | 1.16x (derived 1.167) |
| Apply | 5% → 10% | **2x** |
| Schedule | 50% → 55% | 1.1x |
[derived] Overall conversion = 0.30 × 0.05 × 0.50 = **0.75%** before changes. "Improve each by 5" means +5 **percentage points**.
- [FLAG] The rule's wording is the biggest absolute drop-off; by count opt-in loses more (70 vs 28.5 per 100), yet the book labels Apply (lowest rate) the constraint. [our note] Treat "constraint" = lowest step conversion, then judge effort separately.
- **Protocol:** test **one thing per week per platform** (four reasons: learnability, steps interact, prioritize big tests, don't waste time). Every Monday run one split test per platform; the next Monday pick winners, **write results in a log of all tests**, plan the next test. **If you can't beat the current best in four tries (or one month), move on to the next constraint.**
- [FLAG] "One week is long enough" is the author's experience with his spend. Test length depends on traffic; the book gives no sample-size rule.

## Test and learn budget (LD-097)
Quote: set aside a share of your advertising budget, whether 1%, 5%, or 10%, for trying new things with no return expected. "You either win or you learn." [claim] Case: June $400,000 → July $780,000 → … → past $4M/month, then "$16,000,000 per month" across the portfolio; text blasts and an affiliate program were key steps. One business path; **not an expected outcome.** [our note] No rule for choosing within 1–10%; text blasts need consent. Related paid-ad test budget (2x first-30-day cash): concept 16.

## The volume lesson (LD-098, [claim])
300 flyers → only a complaint; the mentor tests with **5,000** and, on a winner, puts out **5,000 per day for a month**. Benchmark [stated by the mentor]: flyers: half a percent is decent and one percent is a winner; with 300 flyers half a percent is "one and a half people." Ratios: "1/1500th the level of effort"; "100 people over six weeks" vs "100 per day" = **1/42** ([derived] 6 weeks = 42 days ✓).
[FLAG] The "1/1500th" has no worked calculation (300 vs 5,000/day for 90 days gives 1/1500; the text says a month, which gives 1/500). **Do not use it as a computed figure.** [our note] The 0.5%/1% flyer rates are for cold offline flyers; not the same as email/phone/ad rates.

## Open to goal (LD-099)
The commitment is to keep working until a specific number of outcomes is reached, whatever happens. Origin: sales managers had to sign **five new members per day**; finish by lunch or work 18 hours. Contrast: Rule of 100 = fixed **actions**; open to goal = fixed **outcomes**. Drop the notion of simply doing your best; do what is required instead.
[claim] Author's habits: wake 4–5 am; no meetings until noon; 8 hours straight on advertising. [our note] Needs a defined outcome and a sustainable stopping rule; the number of actions depends on the response rate, which must come from data (concepts 13–16).

## Related testing rules elsewhere
- Name testing: write several, test two or three, keep the winner as control (OF-077, LD-009; concept 10).
- Cold scripts: "first 100 conversations or 10,000 emails" before tweaking (LD-042; concept 15).
- Content: control inputs (posting and ask cadence), measure outputs monthly (LD-038; concept 14).

## Do not
- Do not call a test "failed" below the volume that gives a readable result.
- Do not use 0.75%, 1.16x, 5-point improvements as forecasts.
- Do not state "1/1500th" as a computed figure.
