# Concept 04 · The Value Equation

**Books:** Offers (Ch.6, the home of the idea). Leads reuses it as a checklist for offers, ads, affiliates and referrals.
**Built from entries:** OF-025, OF-026, OF-027, OF-028, OF-029, OF-030, OF-031 · LD-020, LD-054, LD-072, LD-088.
**Tag key:** [stated] · [figure] · [derived] · [FLAG] · [claim] · [our note].

## The formula
```
            Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value  =  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
            Time Delay  ×  Effort & Sacrifice
```
- Basis is the **figure** (OF-025). The text only says there are four drivers, two on top to increase and two on the bottom to decrease, and "a division equation and not an addition." The multiplication signs are visible only in the image.

| Variable | Goal | Customer's question [stated] |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | increase | What will I make? |
| Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | increase | How will I know it's going to happen? |
| Perceived Time Delay | decrease | How long will it take? |
| Perceived Effort & Sacrifice | decrease | What is expected of me? |

## Rules the author states
1. **Perception is reality.** A lever counts only as the prospect perceives it. Anything the prospect does not perceive has no value, even if true. (OF-025)
2. **Get the bottom to zero.** If time delay and effort/sacrifice reach zero, value is "infinite" however small the top is; the author calls it the hypothetical limit, "technically undefined." This is why it is a division. (OF-025)
3. The top two (bigger promises, social proof) are "easy" and "less unique"; the bottom two are "harder and more competitive"; the best companies focus on the bottom. (OF-025)
4. The four are hard to separate in practice: "most vehicles combine many of these elements together." (OF-025)

## Not in the book (do not build in)
- A numeric scale for the equation's inputs. **No 1–10 scale, no "max 100."**
- Weights between inputs · a threshold for "good" value · a maximum output.
- The only scoring in the book is OF-030's separate 0/1 **additive** example (see below). It is not the equation's scale.
- [our note] Because the denominator can reach zero, a tool must guard against division by zero and say plainly that any result is a **relative comparison** (offer A vs offer B), not an absolute number.

## The four drivers in detail
**Dream Outcome (OF-026)**
- The expression of the feelings and experiences the prospect has envisioned; the distance between where they are now and what they dream of. Goal is to **channel** existing desire, not create it.
- Status = how much a person's standing, compared with others, is seen to go up or down, socially or professionally.
- Rule: the dream outcome matters most **when comparing different desires**; the one that most directly increases status is valued most. When two products satisfy the **same** desire, their dream outcomes "cancel out" and the price difference comes from the other three drivers.
- Copy principle: frame benefits as **status gained in others' eyes**.

**Perceived Likelihood of Achievement (OF-027)**
- The buyer asks how likely they think it is that this purchase will deliver the result they want. "People pay for certainty."
- Raised by: messaging, proof, what you include or exclude in the offer, and guarantees (see concept 09).

**Time Delay (OF-028)**
- Time between buying and receiving the promised benefit. Two elements: long-term outcome (what they buy) and short-term experience (milestones that keep them).
- Practice: an early emotional win close to the purchase. Gym clients get ads live and close a first $2,000 sale within **7 days**. [stated as one business's practice, not a rule of the framework]
- "Fast beats free": people pay for speed; if competing against free, "double down on speed." [stated]
- [claim] Early winners continue more ("backed by science," no citation).

**Effort & Sacrifice (OF-029)**
- Ancillary costs, tangible and intangible. [figure] Fitness lists 12 items (+ "Etc…"); liposuction lists 3.
- "Done for you" costs more than "do it yourself" because the buyer avoids effort; an expert is also believed more likely to succeed.
- Decreasing **perceived** effort "can massively boost the appeal."

## The author's own scoring example (OF-030) and why not to merge it
Text: each lever rated **0 or 1** ("1 being value achieved, 0 being missing"), the four **added**.
[figure] Meditation vs. Xanax (same dream outcome): Meditation = 1 + 0 + **.5** + 0 = **1.5/4**; Xanax = 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = **4/4**.
- [FLAG] Text says binary; the figure scores one lever **.5**.
- [FLAG] The scoring **adds**; the equation **multiplies and divides**. Not the same operation.
- [FLAG] Scores are **direction-normalized**: for Time Delay and Effort & Sacrifice, **1 = favorable** (fast, easy). A tool must make this explicit.
- [FLAG] Scores are the author's judgment calls with a one-line reason each; no scoring rules are given.
- [claim] Supplements $123B vs health clubs $62B (dated, unverified).

## Perception vs. logic (OF-031)
- Logical fixes have usually already been tried, precisely because they are logical, so psychological ones are what is left. Examples [claim, anecdotes from Rory Sutherland]: London tunnel "next train" map, elevator mirrors, "make fewer and raise the price."
- The claim that perception can matter more than changing the real thing rests on anecdotes only. [our note]

## How Leads reuses it
- **Making an offer by message (LD-020):** a four-row table (what to put in the message for each element) and a stated goal to raise the first two elements and lower the other two. No formula. Short form: a one-line pitch that names the ideal customer, the dream outcome, the time period, how effort and sacrifice are avoided, and a higher perceived likelihood of achievement.
  - Image-only Pro Tip box: **11 ways to increase perceived likelihood** (own proof; proof from people like them; volume of reviews; certifications; stats/research; experts vouching; a new/unique characteristic; celebrity endorsement; guarantee; describing their pain specifically; live or recorded demonstration).
  - Ideal pitch limit: come as near to that as possible, with no lying or overstating.
  - [FLAG] The script line claiming a named woman just worked with you is honest only if the result exists. A tool must not invent testimonials. "Five case studies… that's all I can handle" is a scarcity claim, true only if the cap is real.
- **Ad copy (LD-054, What-Who-When):** eight elements in the figure: more = Dream, Fast, Easy, Likely; less = Nightmare, Slow, Hard, Risky. [FLAG] The text's table pairs Time Delay/Effort (bad variables) with Speed/Ease (their opposites), so the column headings are reversed for rows 3–4. Use the figure's arrangement. The Offers equation has **four** variables, not eight. [derived] Angles = 8 elements × number of "who" perspectives × 3 time frames (5 perspectives = 120); the book does not count them. Ethics flag: "nightmare" is a fear appeal; limit to true, supportable consequences.
- **Affiliate offer (LD-088):** value template for a partner: dream outcome (make more money from current customers) · perceived likelihood (customers already want the product) · effort & sacrifice (no building/delivering/support) · time delay (sell it tomorrow).
- **Referrals (LD-072):** six levers map to ad parts. Levers 2–5 apply the equation. [FLAG] Lever 2 says **lower promises** until the close rate drops, then stop; Offers says raise dream outcome and likelihood. Treat as optimization with a close-rate floor, and the true promise must stay honest. [FLAG] Lever 3's "force new customers to repeat the actions" rests on a correlation (7-day ad + sale → 3x LTGP), not tested causation.

## Cross-book
- Leads' goodwill = value − price (LD-071) restates OF-020's price-to-value (concept 03).
- The "11 ways" list also belongs under guarantees/proof (concept 09).

## Do not
- Do not output a number as "the offer's value."
- Do not use the 0/1 additive example as the equation's scale.
- Do not swap Time Delay/Effort for "Speed/Ease" without flipping the direction.
