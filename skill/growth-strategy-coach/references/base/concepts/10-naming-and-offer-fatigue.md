# Concept 10 · Naming the offer, testing names, and offer fatigue

**Built from entries:** OF-074, OF-075, OF-076, OF-077, OF-078, OF-079, OF-080 · LD-009, LD-029, LD-052, LD-057.
**Tag key:** [stated] · [figure] · [derived] · [FLAG] · [claim] · [our note].

## Why name it (OF-074)
- A Grand Slam Offer earns nothing if no one finds out about it; the name must make ideal prospects interested enough to act. Naming pulls in suitable prospects and pushes away unsuitable ones. [stated]
- Example: "Free Six-Week Stress Release Challenge" vs "Float Tank Center Session": same thing, the first draws more response. [stated]
- [claim] Good naming can bring "2x, 3x, or 10x the response rate" (no source).

## M-A-G-I-C (OF-076, figure)
| Letter | Meaning | Figure wording |
|---|---|---|
| M | Magnet | Make a magnetic reason |
| A | Avatar | Announce the avatar |
| G | Goal | Give them a goal |
| I | Interval | Indicate a time interval |
| C | Container | Complete with a container word |
Rules [stated]:
- **Not all components are mandatory; typically three to five.** Shorter and punchier is better. Components need **not** appear in M-A-G-I-C order. You find out which names work only by writing several down and testing them.
- Theory note: M ≈ attention, A ≈ discrimination, G ≈ purpose, I ≈ timeline, C ≈ method.
Components:
- **Magnetic reason why:** "Why are they making this offer?" Examples: Free, 88% off, Giveaway, seasonal (Spring, Back To School), Grand Opening, New Management, New Building, Anniversary, Halloween, New Year.
- **Avatar:** who it is for (and not for); as specific as possible "but no more"; local markets go **hyper-local** (Towson, not Baltimore).
- **Goal:** the dream outcome in a word or phrase; more specific and tangible is better (Pain Free, Never Out Of Breath, Double Your Profit, First Client, 100k).
- **Interval:** expected duration. **Compliance note** [stated]: most ad platforms will not approve a **quantifiable claim paired with a duration** (it implies a guarantee), e.g., income or weight loss. Use the interval where allowed, or a non-claim goal ("$10,000 in 10 days" vs "Make Your First Sale in 10 Days").
- **Container word:** signals a bundle or system that can't be compared to a commodity (Challenge, Blueprint, Bootcamp, Intensive, Masterclass, Program, Accelerator, Sprint, System, Reset, Workshop).
- Optional polish: rhyme or alliteration ("nice-to-have," "don't force it"). Apply the formula to **sub-items and bonuses** in the stack.
- Example names (author's illustrations): "Free Six-Week Lean-By-Halloween Challenge," "5 Clients in 5 Days Blueprint," "Fill Your Gym in 30 Days (Free!)".
- [our note: truthfulness] The author says the reason-why can be anything at all, provided you believe it. A tool should require the reason to be **true** ("Grand Opening," "New Management," "88% off" must be real).

## Testing names (OF-077)
Generate several; run **two to three of the best** in the ad campaign; **note the winner and use it as the control**; test new names against it. The author has "no idea why some names win," so "don't be emotional." [stated]

## The Leads testing method and data (LD-009)
- Test order [stated]: **headline, image(s), subheadline**; the headline is most important; if you test one thing, test that.
- Cheap methods: polls (a small number of votes is enough to show a rough direction), posting on every platform and asking for "1" or "2", messaging people. If people ask when they can get it, "you have a mega winner."
- Results [figure; poll percentages, no vote counts]: Headline R1 Advertising 69 / Promotion 31 · R2 Advertising 42 / Leads 58 · R3 Marketing 46 / Leads 54 · Subhead R1 45/55 · R2 29/71 · R3 33/67 · R4 30/70 (all favored the subhead about getting strangers interested in buying from you).
- [claim] Ogilvy "80 cents of your dollar"; "five times more people read your headline"; "2x, 3x, or 10x your engagement" (no source for 5x or 2–10x).
- [FLAG] The image test's check mark sits before "Real" but the published cover is the cartoon one; do not cite it as evidence about imagery.
- [our note] No sample sizes or audience description; margins as small as 54/46 and 55/45. **Directional polls, not benchmarks.**
- Headline writing (LD-029): the seven headline components; [FLAG] "at least two" in the text vs "one or more" in the Action Step. LD-052: in an ad "the headline is the first sale."

## Offer fatigue and the wrapper (OF-075, figure)
- Figure: "Same bundle, different wrapper": original bundle (Magical Offer #1), then "Fall 6 wk Challenge" (#2) and "Spring 28 Day Blueprint" (#3).
- Offers fatigue over time, **faster in local markets**; renaming the wrapper "will get you leads forever." Don't change the actual offer, only name and presentation. [stated]
- Fatigue is about "years of use, not months." Reaching an audience once "in no way means an offer is fatigued"; refresh creative, hooks, stories, copy first. [stated]
- Local reach example: on most platforms about $20 reaches 1,000 people; 200,000 people "would only cost you $10,000."
- [FLAG] 200,000 ÷ 1,000 × $20 = **$4,000**, not $10,000. Use the formula (people ÷ 1,000 × cost per 1,000), not the text's total.

## What to change when it fatigues (OF-078)
Order, lightest and cheapest first [stated]: 1 **Creative** · 2 **Body copy** · 3 **Headline / "wrapper"** · 4 **Duration** · 5 **Enhancer** (the free or discount component) · 6 **Monetization structure**, series of offers, and price points.
- Items near the top get changed repeatedly without any need to touch what sits at the bottom; the lower, the more operationally heavy; once an offer is monetized, it should seldom be changed. Change the machine only as a last resort and with a strong reason.
- [FLAG] The closing paragraph adds "seasonality" (order: wrapper, seasonality, duration, free/discount, machine), which is not one of the six. "Wrapper" is used two ways (headline only vs. look and feel = items 1–3).
- Traction [stated]: pick the offer structure and headline you think has the highest chance and stick with it; keep the highest-return one.

## Local vs national (OF-079)
Local is easier to get working (trust in the familiar, in-person selling, higher conversion) but harder to scale: "the smaller the radius, the faster offers fatigue." Practice: rapidly vary offers, headlines and creative without changing the value stack. Rolling seasonal urgency is the author's #1 local strategy (concept 07).

## Section IV recap (OF-080)
You made the offer valuable, rebundled services, added a **guarantee**, **urgency and scarcity**, and **named** it. Bonuses (Ch.14) are not mentioned in this recap.

## Cross-book
- Leads' first-ad case (LD-057) contains M-A-G-I-C elements: Avatar ("Chino Hills residents"), scarcity cap ("5"), free offer, duration ("6 week"), container ("challenge"), plus a stated reason. [FLAG] LD-057: the ad says "FREE 6 WEEK CHALLENGE" and "5 CHINO HILLS RESIDENTS," yet 19 people bought at $299 each; the book never explains how a "free" challenge produced $299 sales, and the cap of "5" did not hold. Do not model the ad's "5" as a real cap, and do not treat "free" as the sold price.

## Do not
- Do not put a quantifiable claim next to a duration in ad-platform copy.
- Do not use a made-up magnetic reason.
- Do not use poll percentages as benchmarks.
