## What it is
Advertising one-to-one (private) to people who don't know you. Any channel that lets you contact a person one-to-one can be used for cold outreach. It "sits atop the foundation of warm outreach." Use it to **grow faster**, **increase predictability of lead flow**, or when you run out of warm people. [stated, LD-040]
"Cold outreach is a numbers game": once you know how much outreach engages a lead, "we only have one thing to do… more."

## The three strangers' problems, in order (LD-040)
| # | Problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | You can't contact them | Build a list |
| 2 | They ignore you | Personalize, then give big fast value |
| 3 | They're not interested | Volume (more outreach, more types, more follow-ups) |
[our note] These are three failure points a tool can ask about; the book gives no numeric split among them.

## Problem 1: build a list (LD-041)
Three sources, most to least accessible: **software** (scraping tools; test "a few hundred from each"; check contact info is current and matches the type) → **brokers** (several; test sample lists; keep what works) → **elbow grease** (join groups/communities; use directories; prefer contact info outside the group). Freshness rule: any database you can search is open to everyone else as well; self-built lists are freshest but slowest. Action: put together your first **1,000 names**; with more time than money, you may want to begin at step three, since it costs only time.
[our note: compliance] The book says "scrape" and buy lists without discussing consent or data rules. **Do not assume scraped or bought contacts are lawful to contact.** Risk area (client data).

## Problem 2: personalize and give big fast value (LD-042)
- **Personalize: "act like you know them":** one to three pieces of information a friend might know, complement them, show how it benefited you; in subject lines, first messages, first sentences.
- **"Giving away the farm":** "blow their minds in under thirty seconds." Offer, lead magnet, or both. Rule: keep raising the offer until saying no would feel foolish; "stuff they actually pay for," not "so good they should pay for it."
- [claim] Swapping a "game planning session" (a sales call) for the largest amount of free service they could afford to give made take rates "3x'd." One case.
- Scripts: phone/chat "never more than a page or two," cold emails "rarely more than half a page"; work through 100 conversations or 10,000 emails before making any tweaks.
- [FLAG] The book promises three sample scripts at the end of the chapter (call, email, chat); **they are not in this copy.** Do not cite scripts from this book.

## Problem 3: volume (LD-043)
| Lever | Manual | Automated |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery | live script by phone; personal voice memo; handwritten letters | pre-recorded voice memo/voicemail/video; templated email or text |
| Distribution | dial each number; send each message | robot dials multiple numbers; blast of 1,000 emails/texts/voicemails |
- Trade-off: "you sacrifice personalization for scale"; the smaller your lead pool, the less you should automate (1,000 hedge fund managers: personalize each; tens of millions of women 25–45: less). Automation = more engaged leads per unit time even if lower percentage.
- Technology rule: give 10 to 20 percent of your effort to new, untested technology.
- Follow up **more than once, in more than one way**; expect **two to three conversations** before a higher-ticket sale. After finishing the list, **start at the top again after three to six months**. (Warm outreach uses up to 3 attempts over 3 days; cold daily checklist uses 2x, 2x, then 1x on day 7: different loops. [our note])
- [FLAG: compliance] The book encourages automated dialing, text blasts and voicemail drops "when ethical and available" and notes "still need to be FTC compliant"; it names no consent rules for texts, automated calls or commercial email. **Risk area.**

## Cost model: cold calling team (LD-045, worked example) [stated, all lines recompute ✓]
Inputs: rep $15/hour + $50 per shown appointment; $3,600 profit per sale; leads $0.10; 200 calls/day; about 2 shows/day; 8-hour day; 33% of shows close.
Labor $120 + commissions $100 + leads $20 = **$240/rep/day** → $120 per show → ≈ **$360** per client → **10:1** ($3,600 ÷ $360).
- [FLAG] The text says the $360 is "excluding commissions," but $120 per show **includes** the $100 commissions. Excluding them: $70 per show, ≈ $212 per client. Treat $360 as including commissions.
- [our note] 200 calls → 2 shows is assumed; the phone benchmark above yields engaged leads, not shows.

## Seven benefits and "solve for X" (LD-046)
Benefits (author's): no need for lots of content/ads; competitors can't see it; **reliable** (a set amount of input produces a set number of responses); fewer platform changes; compliance less painful (FTC still applies; platforms ban tobacco, firearms, cannabis, weight loss); no spokesperson needed (helped the Gym Launch sale); hard to copy.
**Equation:** if contacting X people yields Y customers; after that, solve for X. Example: 100 emails → 1 customer; 100 customers needs **10,000 emails**; ÷ 30 days = **333/day**; one person sends 111/day → **3 people**. [derived ✓] Assumes a flat 1% and a 30-day month.
Warning: most people greatly underestimate the volume needed and the time it takes.

## Origin case (LD-047, [claim])
Monthly sales (each ≈ $16,000 revenue): Sept 0 · Oct 2 · Dec 4 · Jan 6 · Feb 10 · Mar 14 · Apr 20 · May 30 (November not listed). [derived] growth: Jan→Feb +67%, Feb→Mar +40%, Mar→Apr +43%, Apr→May +50%. "It took almost a year" (the hire expected twelve weeks). One company, one ramp: **not a forecast.**

## Daily checklist and "count in 100s" (LD-048, figure)
100 per day · 7 days a week · follow-up cadence of 2x on Day 1, 2x on Day 2, and 1x on Day 7. Pro Tip (image-only): do not set a daily goal under 100, and keep going for at least 100 days. [derived] 100 × 100 = 10,000, matching the 10,000-email test volume. "I promise" is a [claim].
